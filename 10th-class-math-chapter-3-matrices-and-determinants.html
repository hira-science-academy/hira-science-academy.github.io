3.1 – Introduction to Matrices

Definition, order, types, equal matrices

New 2026↑

This section introduces the concept of matrices as rectangular arrays of numbers. You'll learn about rows, columns, order of a matrix, types of matrices (row, column, square, rectangular, diagonal, scalar, identity, symmetric, skew-symmetric), and conditions for matrix equality.

3.2 – Types & Transpose of Matrices

Row, column, square, diagonal, scalar, identity, symmetric, skew-symmetric, transpose

New 2026↓

This section covers the transpose of a matrix (interchanging rows and columns), symmetric matrices \((A^t = A)\), skew-symmetric matrices \((A^t = -A)\), and the negative of a matrix. You'll also practice identifying different types of matrices.

3.3 – Algebraic Operations on Matrices

Scalar multiplication, addition, subtraction, additive identity & inverse

New 2026↓

This section covers scalar multiplication, addition and subtraction of matrices (conformability), commutative and associative laws of addition, additive identity (zero matrix), and additive inverse of a matrix.

3.4 – Multiplication of Matrices

Matrix multiplication, conformability, associative & distributive laws

New 2026↓

This section covers multiplication of matrices, conformability condition (columns of first = rows of second), commutative law (which does not generally hold), associative law \((AB)C = A(BC)\), distributive laws \(A(B+C) = AB + AC\), and multiplicative identity matrix.

3.5 – Multiplicative Inverse of a Matrix

Determinant, singular/non-singular matrices, adjoint, inverse, verification

New 2026↓

This section covers the determinant of a \(2\times2\) matrix, singular and non-singular matrices, adjoint of a matrix, and finding the multiplicative inverse using the adjoint method. You'll also verify that \(AA^{-1} = A^{-1}A = I\).

3.6 – Solution of Simultaneous Linear Equations

Matrix inversion method, Cramer's rule, real-world applications

New 2026↓

This section covers solving systems of linear equations using the matrix inversion method and Cramer's rule. Applications include electrical circuits, forces, support beams, 2D game worlds, ages, cyclists, and fractions.
