📌 Important Revision Points

  • Probability of an event: \(P(A) = \dfrac{n(A)}{n(S)}\), where \(n(A)\) is the number of favourable outcomes and \(n(S)\) is the size of the sample space.
  • Range: \(0 \leq P(A) \leq 1\) for every event \(A\).
  • Complementary rule: \(P(A') = 1 - P(A)\).
  • Single die: \(n(S) = 6\), so each face has probability \(1/6\).
  • Pair of dice: \(n(S) = 36\) ordered pairs \((i,j)\).
  • Standard deck of cards: \(n(S) = 52\); four suits of 13 cards each; 26 red and 26 black.
  • Coin + die: \(n(S) = 2 \times 6 = 12\).

🎲 Exercise 13.1 – Questions & Solutions

Q1 Arshad rolls a dice with sides labelled \(L, M, N, O, P, U\). What is the probability that the dice lands on a consonant?
Solution

When the dice is rolled, the sample space is:

\(S = \{L, M, N, O, P, U\},\quad n(S) = 6\)

Let \(A\) be the event of getting a consonant. The vowels among the letters are \(O\) and \(U\); the consonants are \(L, M, N, P\).

\(A = \{L, M, N, P\},\quad n(A) = 4\)
\(P(A) = \dfrac{n(A)}{n(S)} = \dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
✅ \(P(\text{consonant}) = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
Q2 Shazia throws a pair of fair dice. Find the probability of getting:
(i) Sum of dots at least 4
(ii) Product of both dots between 5 and 10
(iii) Difference between both dots equal to 4
(iv) Number at least 5 on the first dice and number at least 4 on the second dice
Solution

When two dice are thrown, the sample space is the set of all 36 ordered pairs \((i, j)\) with \(i, j \in \{1, \ldots, 6\}\):

\(n(S) = 6 \times 6 = 36\)

(i) Sum of dots is at least 4

Sum at least 4 means sum \(\geq 4\). The complement is sum \(\leq 3\), which occurs only for \((1,1), (1,2), (2,1)\) — 3 outcomes.

\(n(A) = 36 - 3 = 33,\quad P(A) = \dfrac{33}{36} = \dfrac{11}{12}\)

(ii) Product of both dots is between 5 and 10

List the pairs whose product is \(> 5\) and \(< 10\):

\(B = \{(1,6),(2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,3),(4,2),(5,1),(5,2),(6,1)\}\)
\(n(B) = 9,\quad P(B) = \dfrac{9}{36} = \dfrac{1}{4}\)

Note: the textbook list includes \((1,5)\) but its product is 5, which is not strictly greater than 5, so we exclude it here. If your textbook treats "between 5 and 10" as inclusive, add \((1,5)\) and \((2,5)\) back and get \(11/36\).

(iii) Difference between both dots equals 4

\(C = \{(1,5),(2,6),(5,1),(6,2)\},\quad n(C) = 4\)
\(P(C) = \dfrac{4}{36} = \dfrac{1}{9}\)

(iv) First die at least 5 and second die at least 4

\(D = \{(5,4),(5,5),(5,6),(6,4),(6,5),(6,6)\},\quad n(D) = 6\)
\(P(D) = \dfrac{6}{36} = \dfrac{1}{6}\)
✅ (i) 11/12    (ii) 1/4 (or 11/36 if inclusive)    (iii) 1/9    (iv) 1/6
Q3 One alphabet is selected at random from the word "MATHEMATICS". Find the probability of getting:
(i) a vowel   (ii) a consonant   (iii) an \(E\)   (iv) an \(A\)   (v) not \(M\)   (vi) not \(T\)
Solution

The sample space is the set of the 11 letters in MATHEMATICS (counting the repeated letters):

\(S = \{M, A, T, H, E, M, A, T, I, C, S\},\quad n(S) = 11\)

(i) A vowel

\(A = \{A, E, A, I\},\quad n(A) = 4,\quad P(A) = \dfrac{4}{11}\)

(ii) A consonant

\(B = \{M, T, H, M, T, C, S\},\quad n(B) = 7,\quad P(B) = \dfrac{7}{11}\)

(iii) An \(E\)

\(C = \{E\},\quad n(C) = 1,\quad P(C) = \dfrac{1}{11}\)

(iv) An \(A\)

\(D = \{A, A\},\quad n(D) = 2,\quad P(D) = \dfrac{2}{11}\)

(v) Not \(M\)

There are two \(M\)'s, so \(P(M) = \dfrac{2}{11}\).

\(P(M') = 1 - P(M) = 1 - \dfrac{2}{11} = \dfrac{9}{11}\)

(vi) Not \(T\)

There are two \(T\)'s, so \(P(T) = \dfrac{2}{11}\).

\(P(T') = 1 - \dfrac{2}{11} = \dfrac{9}{11}\)
✅ (i) 4/11    (ii) 7/11    (iii) 1/11    (iv) 2/11    (v) 9/11    (vi) 9/11
Q4 Aslam rolls a dice. What is the probability of getting the numbers 3 or 4? Also find the probability of not getting the numbers 3 or 4.
Solution
\(S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\},\quad n(S) = 6\)

Let \(A\) be the event of getting 3 or 4:

\(A = \{3, 4\},\quad n(A) = 2\)
\(P(A) = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}\)

Using the complementary rule:

\(P(A') = 1 - P(A) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
✅ \(P(3\text{ or }4) = \dfrac{1}{3}\), \(P(\text{not }3\text{ or }4) = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
Q5 Abdul Hadi labelled cards from 1 to 30 and put them in a box. He selects a card at random. What is the probability that the selected card contains:
(i) the number 25
(ii) a number between 17 and 22
(iii) a number at least 20
(iv) a number not 27 and 29
(v) a number not between 12 and 15
Solution
\(S = \{1, 2, \ldots, 30\},\quad n(S) = 30\)

(i) The number 25

\(A = \{25\},\quad n(A) = 1,\quad P(A) = \dfrac{1}{30}\)

(ii) A number between 17 and 22

\(B = \{17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22\},\quad n(B) = 6\)
\(P(B) = \dfrac{6}{30} = \dfrac{1}{5}\)

(iii) A number at least 20

\(C = \{20, 21, \ldots, 30\},\quad n(C) = 11,\quad P(C) = \dfrac{11}{30}\)

(iv) A number not 27 and 29

The event "27 and 29" means either of those two numbers:

\(D = \{27, 29\},\quad n(D) = 2,\quad P(D) = \dfrac{2}{30} = \dfrac{1}{15}\)
\(P(D') = 1 - \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{14}{15}\)

(v) A number not between 12 and 15

\(E = \{12, 13, 14, 15\},\quad n(E) = 4,\quad P(E) = \dfrac{4}{30} = \dfrac{2}{15}\)
\(P(E') = 1 - \dfrac{2}{15} = \dfrac{13}{15}\)
✅ (i) 1/30    (ii) 1/5    (iii) 11/30    (iv) 14/15    (v) 13/15
Q6 The probability that Ayesha will pass the examination is \(0.85\). What will be the probability that Ayesha will not pass the examination?
Solution

Let \(A\) be the event that Ayesha passes the examination.

\(P(A) = 0.85\)
\(P(A') = 1 - P(A) = 1 - 0.85 = 0.15\)
✅ \(P(\text{not pass}) = 0.15\)
Q7 Taabish tossed a fair coin and rolled a fair dice once. Find the probability of the following events:
(i) tail on coin and at least 4 on dice
(ii) head on coin and the number 2 or 3 on dice
(iii) head and tail on coin and the number 6 on dice
(iv) not tail on coin and the number 5 on dice
(v) not head on coin and the numbers 5 and 2 on dice
Solution

When a coin and a die are tossed together, the sample space is:

\(S = \{(H,1),(H,2),(H,3),(H,4),(H,5),(H,6),(T,1),(T,2),(T,3),(T,4),(T,5),(T,6)\}\)
\(n(S) = 12\)

(i) Tail on coin and at least 4 on dice

\(A = \{(T,4),(T,5),(T,6)\},\quad n(A) = 3,\quad P(A) = \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{1}{4}\)

(ii) Head on coin and 2 or 3 on dice

\(B = \{(H,2),(H,3)\},\quad n(B) = 2,\quad P(B) = \dfrac{2}{12} = \dfrac{1}{6}\)

(iii) Head and tail on coin, and 6 on dice

"Head and tail" means either \(H\) or \(T\) (both coin outcomes), combined with 6 on the die:

\(C = \{(H,6),(T,6)\},\quad n(C) = 2,\quad P(C) = \dfrac{2}{12} = \dfrac{1}{6}\)

(iv) Not tail on coin and 5 on dice

Not tail \(= H\), so:

\(D = \{(H,5)\},\quad n(D) = 1,\quad P(D) = \dfrac{1}{12}\)
\(P(\text{not tail and }5) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{11}{12}\)

(v) Not head on coin and the numbers 5 and 2 on dice

"Not head" means \(T\). "The numbers 5 and 2" means either 5 or 2:

\(E = \{(H,5),(H,2)\},\quad n(E) = 2,\quad P(E) = \dfrac{2}{12} = \dfrac{1}{6}\)
\(P(E') = 1 - \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{5}{6}\)
✅ (i) 1/4    (ii) 1/6    (iii) 1/6    (iv) 11/12    (v) 5/6
Q8 A card is selected at random from a well-shuffled pack of 52 playing cards. What is the probability of selecting:
(i) a queen
(ii) neither a queen nor a jack
Solution

Total number of cards \(= 52\), so \(n(S) = 52\).

(i) A queen

There are 4 queens in the deck:

\(n(Q) = 4,\quad P(Q) = \dfrac{4}{52} = \dfrac{1}{13}\)

(ii) Neither a queen nor a jack

"Queen or jack" has 4 + 4 = 8 cards:

\(n(Q \cup J) = 8,\quad P(Q \cup J) = \dfrac{8}{52} = \dfrac{2}{13}\)
\(P(\text{neither Q nor J}) = 1 - \dfrac{2}{13} = \dfrac{11}{13}\)
✅ (i) 1/13    (ii) 11/13
Q9 A card is chosen at random from a pack of 52 playing cards. Find the probability of getting:
(i) a jack    (ii) no diamond
Solution

Total number of cards \(= 52\), so \(n(S) = 52\).

(i) A jack

There are 4 jacks in the deck:

\(n(J) = 4,\quad P(J) = \dfrac{4}{52} = \dfrac{1}{13}\)

(ii) No diamond

There are 13 diamonds in the deck:

\(n(D) = 13,\quad P(D) = \dfrac{13}{52} = \dfrac{1}{4}\)
\(P(D') = 1 - \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{4}\)
✅ (i) 1/13    (ii) 3/4

💡 Exam Tip:

Always write out the sample space (or at least state \(n(S)\)) before calculating. For "at least" questions, it is usually faster to work out the complement (e.g. \(P(\text{sum} \geq 4) = 1 - P(\text{sum} \leq 3)\)) — this avoids long enumerations. For pair-of-dice problems, remember \(n(S) = 36\); for a single die, \(n(S) = 6\); for a standard deck of cards, \(n(S) = 52\) with 4 suits of 13 and 26 red plus 26 black. Show the reduction of the fraction wherever possible — an unsimplified fraction like \(4/6\) is often worth one less mark.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions – Exercise 13.1

Exercise 13.1 covers probability of single events using dice, playing cards, coins and words. Students find the sample space, list favorable outcomes, and compute \(P(A) = \dfrac{n(A)}{n(S)}\), then apply the complementary rule \(P(A') = 1 - P(A)\).

The complete PDF is embedded near the top of this page and can also be opened with the "⬇️ Download PDF – Exercise 13.1 Solutions (Free)" button directly below the PDF viewer.

If \(A\) is an event and \(A'\) is the event that \(A\) does not occur, then \(P(A) + P(A') = 1\), so \(P(A') = 1 - P(A)\). For example, if the probability of passing an exam is 0.85, the probability of not passing is \(1 - 0.85 = 0.15\).

A standard die has 6 faces, so \(n(S) = 6\). A standard deck of playing cards has 52 cards, so \(n(S) = 52\), with 4 suits of 13 cards each and 26 red plus 26 black cards. The probability formula \(P(A) = \dfrac{n(A)}{n(S)}\) is the same for both — only the sample space size differs.
