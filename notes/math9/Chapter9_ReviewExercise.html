📌 Important Revision Points

  • Similar Figures: Same shape, different size. Corresponding angles are equal, corresponding sides are proportional.
  • Scale Factor: Ratio of corresponding sides. Used to enlarge or reduce figures.
  • Area Ratio: Ratio of areas = (scale factor)².
  • Volume Ratio: Ratio of volumes = (scale factor)³.
  • Finding sides from area ratio: Take the square root of the area ratio.
  • Finding sides from volume ratio: Take the cube root of the volume ratio.
  • Tessellation: A shape tiles the plane without gaps or overlaps if the angles meeting at each vertex sum to \(360^\circ\).

📐 Review Exercise – Questions & Solutions

Q2 If the sum of the interior angles of a polygon is \(1080^\circ\), how many sides does the polygon have?
Solution
\(Sum\ of\ the\ interior\ angles\ of\ a\ polygon = 1080^\circ\)
\(Number\ of\ sides = n = ?\)
\(Sum\ of\ interior\ angles = 180^\circ(n-2)\)
\(1080^\circ = 180^\circ(n-2)\)
\(\dfrac{1080^\circ}{180^\circ} = n-2\)
\(6 = n-2\)
\(n = 8\)
✅ Thus, the polygon has 8 sides.
Q3 Two similar bottles are such that one is twice as high as the other. What is the ratio of their surface areas and their capacities?
Solution

Let:

\(Height\ of\ second\ bottle = h_2 = h\)
\(Height\ of\ first\ bottle = h_1 = 2h\)

If \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) be the surface areas of first and second bottles respectively. Using formula of area for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\dfrac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^2\)
\(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\dfrac{2h}{h}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{2}{1}\right)^2 = \dfrac{4}{1}\)
\(A_1 : A_2 = 4 : 1\)

Let \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) be the capacities (volumes) of these bottles. Using formula of volumes for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{C_1}{C_2} = \left(\dfrac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^3\)
\(\dfrac{C_1}{C_2} = \left(\dfrac{2h}{h}\right)^3 = \left(\dfrac{2}{1}\right)^3 = \dfrac{8}{1}\)
\(C_1 : C_2 = 8 : 1\)
✅ Ratio of surface areas = \(4:1\)    Ratio of capacities = \(8:1\)
Q4 Each dimension of a model car is \(\frac{1}{10}\) of the corresponding car dimension. Find the ratio of:
(a) the areas of their windscreens
(b) the capacities of their boots
(c) the widths of the cars
(d) the number of wheels they have
Solution
\(Linear\ scale\ factor = k = \dfrac{Model\ dimension}{Actual\ dimension} = \dfrac{1}{10}\)

(a) the areas of their windscreens

Let \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) be the areas of model and actual car's windscreens. Using formula of areas for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = k^2 = \left(\dfrac{1}{10}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{100}\)
\(A_1 : A_2 = 1 : 100\)

(b) the capacities of their boots

Let \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) be the capacities (volumes) of their boots. Using formula of volumes for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{C_1}{C_2} = k^3 = \left(\dfrac{1}{10}\right)^3 = \dfrac{1}{1000}\)
\(C_1 : C_2 = 1 : 1000\)

(c) the widths of the cars

Let \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) be the widths of model and actual car:

\(\dfrac{W_1}{W_2} = k = \dfrac{1}{10}\)
\(W_1 : W_2 = 1 : 10\)

(d) the number of wheels they have

\(No.\ of\ wheels\ of\ model\ car = 4\)
\(No.\ of\ wheels\ of\ actual\ car = 4\)
\(Ratio\ of\ no.\ of\ wheels = 4 : 4 = 1 : 1\)
✅ (a) \(1:100\)    (b) \(1:1000\)    (c) \(1:10\)    (d) \(1:1\)
Q5 Three similar jugs have heights 8 cm, 12 cm and 16 cm. If the smallest jug holds \(\frac{1}{2}\) litre, find the capacities of the other two.
Solution
\(Height\ of\ first\ jug = h_1 = 8\text{ cm}\)
\(Height\ of\ second\ jug = h_2 = 12\text{ cm}\)
\(Height\ of\ third\ jug = h_3 = 16\text{ cm}\)
\(Volume\ of\ first\ jug = V_1 = \frac{1}{2}\text{ litre}\)
\(Volume\ of\ second\ jug = V_2 = ?\)
\(Volume\ of\ third\ jug = V_3 = ?\)

For volume of second jug \(V_2\) using formula of volumes for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \left(\dfrac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^3\)
\(\dfrac{\frac{1}{2}}{V_2} = \left(\dfrac{8}{12}\right)^3 = \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^3 = \dfrac{8}{27}\)
\(\dfrac{1}{2V_2} = \dfrac{8}{27}\)
\(16V_2 = 27 \Rightarrow V_2 = \dfrac{27}{16} = 1.6875\text{ litre}\)

For volume of third jug \(V_3\) using formula of volumes for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{V_1}{V_3} = \left(\dfrac{h_1}{h_3}\right)^3\)
\(\dfrac{\frac{1}{2}}{V_3} = \left(\dfrac{8}{16}\right)^3 = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \dfrac{1}{8}\)
\(\dfrac{1}{2V_3} = \dfrac{1}{8}\)
\(2V_3 = 8 \Rightarrow V_3 = 4\text{ litre}\)
✅ Capacity of second jug = \(1.6875\text{ litre}\)  |  Capacity of third jug = \(4\text{ litres}\)
Q6 Three similar drinking glasses have heights 7.5 cm, 9 cm and 10.5 cm. If the tallest glass holds 343 millilitres, find the capacities of the other two.
Solution
\(Height\ of\ first\ glass = h_1 = 7.5\text{ cm}\)
\(Height\ of\ second\ glass = h_2 = 9\text{ cm}\)
\(Height\ of\ third\ glass = h_3 = 10.5\text{ cm}\)
\(Volume\ (capacity)\ of\ third\ glass = V_3 = 343\text{ ml}\)
\(Volume\ of\ first\ glass = V_1 = ?\)
\(Volume\ of\ second\ glass = V_2 = ?\)

For capacity of first glass \(V_1\) using formula of volumes for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{V_1}{V_3} = \left(\dfrac{h_1}{h_3}\right)^3\)
\(\dfrac{V_1}{343} = \left(\dfrac{7.5}{10.5}\right)^3 = \left(\dfrac{5}{7}\right)^3 = \dfrac{125}{343}\)
\(V_1 = \dfrac{125}{343} \times 343 = 125\text{ ml}\)

For capacity of second glass \(V_2\) using formula of volumes for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{V_2}{V_3} = \left(\dfrac{h_2}{h_3}\right)^3\)
\(\dfrac{V_2}{343} = \left(\dfrac{9}{10.5}\right)^3 = \left(\dfrac{6}{7}\right)^3 = \dfrac{216}{343}\)
\(V_2 = \dfrac{216}{343} \times 343 = 216\text{ ml}\)
✅ Capacity of first glass = \(125\text{ ml}\)  |  Capacity of second glass = \(216\text{ ml}\)
Q7 A toy manufacturer produces model cars similar to actual cars. The ratio of door area (model to actual) is \(1 : 2500\). Find:
(a) the ratio of their lengths
(b) the ratio of the capacities of their petrol tanks
(c) the width of the model, if the actual car is 150 cm wide
(d) the area of the rear window of the actual car, if the area of the rear window of the model is \(3\text{ cm}^2\)
Solution
\(Ratio\ of\ areas\ of\ doors\ of\ model\ and\ actual\ cars: A_1 : A_2 = 1 : 2500\)

(a) the ratio of their lengths

Let \(l_1\) and \(l_2\) be the lengths of model and actual cars respectively:

\(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\dfrac{l_1}{l_2}\right)^2\)
\(\dfrac{1}{2500} = \left(\dfrac{l_1}{l_2}\right)^2\)
\(\dfrac{l_1}{l_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2500}} = \dfrac{1}{50}\)
\(l_1 : l_2 = 1 : 50\)

(b) the ratio of the capacities of their petrol tanks

Let \(V_1\) and \(V_2\) be the capacities (volumes) of petrol tanks. Using formula of volumes for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \left(\dfrac{l_1}{l_2}\right)^3\)
\(\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \left(\dfrac{1}{50}\right)^3 = \dfrac{1}{125000}\)
\(V_1 : V_2 = 1 : 125000\)

(c) the width of the model, if the actual car is 150 cm wide

Let \(W_1\) and \(W_2 = 150\text{ cm}\) be the widths of model and actual car:

\(\dfrac{W_1}{W_2} = \dfrac{l_1}{l_2}\)
\(\dfrac{W_1}{150} = \dfrac{1}{50}\)
\(W_1 = \dfrac{1}{50} \times 150 = 3\text{ cm}\)

(d) the area of the rear window of the actual car, if the area of the rear window of the model is \(3\text{ cm}^2\)

Let \(A_1 = 3\text{ cm}^2\) and \(A_2\) be the areas of windows of model and actual cars respectively:

\(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\dfrac{l_1}{l_2}\right)^2\)
\(\dfrac{3}{A_2} = \left(\dfrac{1}{50}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{2500}\)
\(A_2 = 3 \times 2500 = 7500\text{ cm}^2\)
✅ (a) \(1:50\)    (b) \(1:125000\)    (c) \(3\text{ cm}\)    (d) \(7500\text{ cm}^2\)
Q8 The ratio of the areas of two similar labels on two similar jars of coffee is \(144:169\). Find the ratio of:
(a) the heights of the two jars
(b) their capacities
Solution
\(Ratio\ of\ areas\ of\ two\ similar\ jars: A_1 : A_2 = 144 : 169\)

(a) the heights of the two jars

Let \(h_1\) and \(h_2\) be the heights of two jars respectively:

\(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\dfrac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^2\)
\(\left(\dfrac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{144}{169}\)
\(\dfrac{h_1}{h_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{144}{169}} = \dfrac{12}{13}\)
\(h_1 : h_2 = 12 : 13\)

(b) their capacities

Let \(V_1\) and \(V_2\) be the capacities (volumes) of jars. Using formula of volumes for similar solids:

\(\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \left(\dfrac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^3\)
\(\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \left(\dfrac{12}{13}\right)^3 = \dfrac{1728}{2197}\)
\(V_1 : V_2 = 1728 : 2197\)
✅ (a) Height ratio = \(12:13\)    (b) Capacity ratio = \(1728:2197\)
Q9 A tessellation of tiles on a floor uses a repeating pattern of 1 regular hexagon, 6 squares and 6 equilateral triangles. Find the total area of a single pattern with side length \(\frac{1}{2}\) metre of each polygon.
Solution

Given 1 regular hexagon, 6 equilateral triangles, and 6 squares.

\(Side\ length\ of\ each\ polygon = s = \dfrac{1}{2}\text{ m}\)

Now:

\(Area\ of\ hexagon = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot s^2 = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{8} \approx 0.65\text{ m}^2\)
\(Area\ of\ equilateral\ triangle = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot s^2 = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{16} \approx 0.108\text{ m}^2\)
\(Area\ of\ square = s^2 = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{4} = 0.25\text{ m}^2\)

Total area of a single pattern:

\(Total\ area = A_{hexagon} + 6A_{triangle} + 6A_{square}\)
\(= 0.65 + 6(0.108) + 6(0.25)\)
\(= 0.65 + 0.648 + 1.5 = 2.8\text{ m}^2\)
✅ Thus, \(2.8\text{ m}^2\) is the total area of a single pattern.

📈 Key Concepts – Similar Figures

  • Similar Figures: Same shape, different size. Corresponding angles are equal, corresponding sides are proportional.
  • Scale Factor: Ratio of corresponding sides. Used to enlarge or reduce figures.
  • Area Ratio: Ratio of areas = (scale factor)².
  • Volume Ratio: Ratio of volumes = (scale factor)³.
  • Finding sides from area ratio: Take the square root of the area ratio.
  • Finding sides from volume ratio: Take the cube root of the volume ratio.
  • Tessellation: A shape tiles the plane without gaps or overlaps if the angles meeting at each vertex sum to \(360^\circ\).

💡 Exam Tip:

In review exercises, questions often combine multiple concepts. Always identify whether you are dealing with lengths, areas, or volumes first. If the question gives you the area ratio and asks for a volume, first find the length ratio (by taking the square root of the area ratio), then cube it. If it gives you the volumes and asks for a side, take the cube root of the volume ratio. For tessellation problems, sketch the pattern if possible, and remember that areas of regular polygons can be added directly.
