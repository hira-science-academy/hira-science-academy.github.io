📌 Important Revision Points

  • Relative frequency: \(\text{R.F.} = \dfrac{f}{N}\), where \(f\) is the frequency of an outcome and \(N\) is the total number of observations.
  • Percentage: \(\dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{total}} \times 100\).
  • Expected frequency: \(E = N \times P(A)\), where \(N\) is the total number of trials and \(P(A)\) is the probability of the event.
  • Probability range: \(0 \leq P(A) \leq 1\).
  • Sum of relative frequencies: always equals \(1\).
  • Sum of expected frequencies: always equals the total number of trials \(N\).

Q4 A survey was conducted with 108 students of a class about their favourite food. The responses are:
Biryani: 40, Fresh Juice: 7, Chicken: 21, Bar. B.Q: 15, Sweets: 25
(i) What percentage of students like biryani?
(ii) What percentage of students like chicken?
(iii) Which food is the least liked by the students?
(iv) Which food is the most preferred by the students?
Solution

Total students \(N = 40 + 7 + 21 + 15 + 25 = 108\).

(i) Percentage of students who like biryani

\(\text{Percentage} = \dfrac{40}{108} \times 100 \approx 37.04\%\)

(ii) Percentage of students who like chicken

\(\text{Percentage} = \dfrac{21}{108} \times 100 \approx 19.44\%\)

(iii) Least liked food

The lowest count is Fresh Juice with 7 students.

(iv) Most preferred food

The highest count is Biryani with 40 students.

✅ (i) ≈ 37.04%    (ii) ≈ 19.44%    (iii) Fresh Juice    (iv) Biryani
Q5 In 500 trials of throwing two dice, what is the expected frequency that the sum will be greater than 8?
Solution

When two dice are thrown, the sample space consists of 36 ordered pairs:

\(n(S) = 36\)

Let \(A\) be the event of getting a sum greater than 8:

\(A = \{(3,6),(4,5),(5,4),(6,3),(4,6),(5,5),(6,4),(5,6),(6,5),(6,6)\}\)
\(n(A) = 10,\quad P(A) = \dfrac{10}{36} = \dfrac{5}{18}\)

Expected frequency \(= N \times P(A)\):

\(E = 500 \times \dfrac{5}{18} \approx 138.9\)
✅ Expected frequency \(\approx 139\) (or \(138.9\)) times
Q6 What is the expectation of a person who is to get Rs. 120 if he obtains at least 2 heads in a single toss of three coins?
Solution

When three coins are tossed, the sample space is:

\(S = \{HHH, HHT, HTH, THH, HTT, THT, TTH, TTT\},\quad n(S) = 8\)

Let \(A\) be the event of getting at least 2 heads:

\(A = \{HHT, HTH, THH, HHH\},\quad n(A) = 4\)
\(P(A) = \dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{1}{2}\)

Expectation \(= \text{Prize} \times P(A)\):

\(E = 120 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = 60\)
✅ Expected value = Rs. 60

Q8 The probability of getting 5 sixes while tossing six dice is \(\dfrac{2}{5}\). If the dice is rolled 200 times, how many times would you expect it to show 5 sixes?
Solution

The probability of getting 5 sixes is \(P(5\text{ sixes}) = \dfrac{2}{5}\), and \(N = 200\) trials.

Expected frequency \(= N \times P(5\text{ sixes})\):

\(E = 200 \times \dfrac{2}{5} = 80\)
✅ Expected \(= 80\) times

💡 Exam Tip:

Always write down the total \(N\) (sum of frequencies) first — most marks are lost for using the wrong total. For relative frequency, the result must be between \(0\) and \(1\) and all the values must sum to \(1\); use that as a check. For expected frequency, remember the formula \(E = N \times P(A)\): find \(P(A)\) from the sample space before you multiply. In pair-of-dice problems, \(n(S) = 36\); in three-coin problems, \(n(S) = 8\); in a probability distribution, the probabilities must already sum to \(1\) — if they do not, re-check your reading of the table.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions – Exercise 13.2

Exercise 13.2 covers relative frequency from a frequency table, percentages of survey responses, and expected frequency using the formula \(E = N \times P(A)\). Questions include dice, coins, defective-product sampling, and a probability table for a random variable.

The complete PDF is embedded near the top of this page and can also be opened with the "⬇️ Download PDF – Exercise 13.2 Solutions (Free)" button directly below the PDF viewer.

Relative frequency is \(\dfrac{f}{N}\) — the frequency of an outcome divided by the total number of observations. Expected frequency is \(N \times P(A)\) — the total number of trials multiplied by the probability of the event. Relative frequency is measured after the experiment; expected frequency is predicted before it.

First find the probability \(P(A)\) of the event using the sample space, then multiply by the total number of trials: \(E = N \times P(A)\). For example, if \(P(\text{sum} > 8 \text{ when two dice are thrown}) = \dfrac{5}{18}\) and there are 500 trials, the expected frequency is \(500 \times \dfrac{5}{18} \approx 139\).
