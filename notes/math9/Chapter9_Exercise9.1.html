📌 Important Revision Points

  • Similar figures: same shape, corresponding angles are equal, corresponding sides are proportional.
  • Similar triangles: \(\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF\) means \(\dfrac{AB}{DE} = \dfrac{BC}{EF} = \dfrac{AC}{DF}\).
  • Ratio of sides: If two solids are similar with side ratio \(a:b\), the ratio is the same for every corresponding pair of sides.
  • Parallel lines in triangles: A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides in the same ratio.
  • Shadow problem: At the same time of day, height : shadow is the same for all vertical objects.
  • Pythagoras: \(H^2 = B^2 + P^2\) — used to find missing sides in right triangles.

📐 Exercise 9.1 – Questions & Solutions

Q1 Find whether the solids are similar. All lengths are in cm.
Solution

Ratio of corresponding sides are given as follows;

\(\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{3}{4.5} = \dfrac{5}{7.5} = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
✅ The solids are similar because their corresponding side lengths are proportional.
Q2 In triangle ABC: \(m\overline{AB}=6\text{ cm}\), \(m\overline{BC}=9\text{ cm}\), \(m\overline{CA}=12\text{ cm}\). In triangle DEF: \(m\overline{DE}=10.5\text{ cm}\), \(m\overline{EF}=15.75\text{ cm}\), \(m\overline{FD}=21\text{ cm}\). Prove that the triangles are similar.
Solution

Ratio of corresponding sides are given as follows;

\(\dfrac{6}{10.5} = \dfrac{9}{15.75} = \dfrac{12}{21} = \dfrac{4}{7}\)

Since the ratios of the corresponding sides are equal

\(\dfrac{m\overline{AB}}{m\overline{DE}} = \dfrac{m\overline{BC}}{m\overline{EF}} = \dfrac{m\overline{AC}}{m\overline{DF}}\)
✅ Therefore \(\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF\). (Proved)
Q3 In the given figure, \(\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF\), \(m\overline{AB}=12\text{ cm}\), \(m\overline{AC}=20\text{ cm}\) and \(m\overline{BC}=16\text{ cm}\). In \(\Delta DEF\), \(m\overline{DE}=6\text{ cm}\). Find \(m\overline{DF}\) and \(m\overline{EF}\).
Solution

As \(\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF\), so corresponding sides have same ratio.

By using

\(\dfrac{m\overline{AB}}{m\overline{DE}} = \dfrac{m\overline{BC}}{m\overline{EF}}\)
\(\dfrac{12}{6} = \dfrac{16}{m\overline{EF}}\)
\((12)(m\overline{EF}) = (16)(6)\)
\(m\overline{EF} = \dfrac{(16)(6)}{12} = \dfrac{96}{12}\)
\(m\overline{EF} = 8\text{ cm}\)

Similarly,

\(\dfrac{m\overline{AB}}{m\overline{DE}} = \dfrac{m\overline{AC}}{m\overline{DF}}\)
\(\dfrac{12}{6} = \dfrac{20}{m\overline{DF}}\)
\((12)(m\overline{DF}) = (20)(6)\)
\(m\overline{DF} = \dfrac{(20)(6)}{12} = \dfrac{120}{12}\)
\(m\overline{DF} = 10\text{ cm}\)
✅ \(m\overline{EF} = 8\text{ cm}\) and \(m\overline{DF} = 10\text{ cm}\)
Q4 Find the value of \(x\) in each of the following.
Solution

(i)

Since the triangles are similar, their corresponding sides are proportional. By using

\(\dfrac{m\overline{AB}}{m\overline{CD}} = \dfrac{m\overline{AE}}{m\overline{CE}}\)
\(\dfrac{x}{1.2} = \dfrac{7.5}{3}\)
\((x)(3) = (7.5)(1.2)\)
\(x = \dfrac{(7.5)(1.2)}{3} = \dfrac{9}{3}\)
\(x = 3\text{ cm}\)

(ii)

Since \(\overline{EF} \parallel \overline{CD}\), the segments of the sides \(\overline{BC}\) and \(\overline{BD}\) are in the same ratio:

\(\dfrac{m\overline{BE}}{m\overline{EC}} = \dfrac{m\overline{BF}}{m\overline{FD}}\)
\(\dfrac{6}{x} = \dfrac{8}{3}\)
\((6)(3) = (8)(x)\)
\(\dfrac{18}{8} = x\)
\(x = 2.25\text{ cm}\)

(iii)

As \(m\angle C = m\angle F\) and \(m\angle CED = m\angle FEG\), so \(\Delta CED \sim \Delta FEG\).

By using

\(\dfrac{m\overline{CD}}{m\overline{FG}} = \dfrac{m\overline{ED}}{m\overline{EG}}\)
\(\dfrac{x}{2.1} = \dfrac{2.5}{2.4}\)
\((x)(2.4) = (2.5)(2.1)\)
\(x = \dfrac{5.25}{2.4}\)
\(x = 2.19\text{ cm}\)
✅ (i) \(x = 3\text{ cm}\)    (ii) \(x = 2.25\text{ cm}\)    (iii) \(x = 2.19\text{ cm}\)
Q5 A plank is placed straight upstairs that is 20 cm wide and 16 cm deep. A rectangular box of height 8 cm and width \(x\) cm is placed on a stair under the plank. Find the value of \(x\).
Solution

From given figure,

Large Triangle: Height = 16 cm and Base = 20 cm

Small Triangle: Height = 8 cm and Base = \((20 - x)\) cm

Since the triangles are similar because they share the same angles, so

\(\dfrac{\text{Base of Small Triangle}}{\text{Base of Large Triangle}} = \dfrac{\text{Height of Small Triangle}}{\text{Height of Large Triangle}}\)
\(\dfrac{20 - x}{20} = \dfrac{8}{16}\)
\((16)(20 - x) = (8)(20)\)
\(320 - 16x = 160\)
\(-16x = 160 - 320\)
\(-16x = -160\)
\(x = \dfrac{-160}{-16}\)
\(x = 10\text{ cm}\)
✅ \(x = 10\text{ cm}\)
Q6 A man who is 1.8 m tall casts a shadow of 0.76 m in length. If at the same time a telephone pole casts a 3 m shadow, find the height of the pole.
Solution

From given data,

Man: Height = 1.8 m and Shadow = 0.76 m

Pole: Height = \(h\) (Unknown) and Shadow = 3 m

Since the triangles are similar because the sun's angle is the same, so

\(\dfrac{\text{Height of Pole}}{\text{Height of Man}} = \dfrac{\text{Shadow of Pole}}{\text{Shadow of Man}}\)
\(\dfrac{h}{1.8} = \dfrac{3}{0.76}\)
\((h)(0.76) = (3)(1.8)\)
\(h = \dfrac{(3)(1.8)}{0.76} = \dfrac{5.4}{0.76}\)
\(h \approx 7.1\text{ m}\)
✅ The height of the telephone pole is approximately 7.1 m.
Q7 Find the values of \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\) in the given figure.
Solution
For \(y\): By using Pythagoras theorem
\((H)^2 = (B)^2 + (P)^2\)
\((10)^2 = (6)^2 + (y)^2\)
\(100 = 36 + y^2\)
\(100 - 36 = y^2\)
\(64 = y^2\)
\(y = \sqrt{64}\)
\(y = 8\text{ cm}\)
For \(x\):

We compare the small top triangle to the large overall triangle. These two triangles are similar, so

\(\dfrac{\text{Large hypotenuse}}{\text{Small hypotenuse}} = \dfrac{\text{Large base}}{\text{Small base}}\)
\(\dfrac{6 + x}{10} = \dfrac{10}{6}\)
\((6)(6 + x) = (10)(10)\)
\(36 + 6x = 100\)
\(6x = 100 - 36\)
\(6x = 64\)
\(x = \dfrac{64}{6}\)
\(x = 10.67\text{ cm}\)
For \(z\): By using Pythagoras theorem
\((H)^2 = (B)^2 + (P)^2\)
\((z)^2 = (8)^2 + (10.67)^2\)
\(z^2 = 64 + 113.85\)
\(z^2 = 177.85\)
\(z = \sqrt{177.85}\)
\(z \approx 13.33\text{ cm}\)
✅ \(y = 8\text{ cm}\), \(x \approx 10.67\text{ cm}\), \(z \approx 13.33\text{ cm}\)
Q8 Draw an isosceles trapezoid \(ABCD\) where \(\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD}\) and \(m\overline{AB} > m\overline{CD}\). Diagonals \(\overline{AC}\) and \(\overline{BD}\) intersect at \(E\). Prove \(\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE\). If \(m\overline{AB}=8\text{ cm}\), \(m\overline{CD}=4\text{ cm}\), and \(m\overline{AE}=3\text{ cm}\), find \(m\overline{CE}\).
Solution

In \(\Delta ABE\) and \(\Delta CDE\), \(\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD}\), so

  • i. \(\angle CDE = \angle ABE\)  (Alternate interior angles)
  • ii. \(\angle DCE = \angle BAE\)  (Alternate interior angles)
  • iii. \(\angle CED = \angle AEB\)  (Vertical angles)

Since three corresponding angles of the triangles are equal, so \(\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE\). (Proved)

As \(\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE\)

\(\dfrac{m\overline{AB}}{m\overline{CD}} = \dfrac{m\overline{AE}}{m\overline{CE}}\)
\(\dfrac{8}{4} = \dfrac{3}{m\overline{CE}}\)
\((8)(m\overline{CE}) = (3)(4)\)
\(m\overline{CE} = \dfrac{12}{8}\)
\(m\overline{CE} = 1.5\text{ cm}\)
✅ \(m\overline{CE} = 1.5\text{ cm}\)
Q9 A regular dodecagon has its side lengths decreased by a factor of \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\). If the perimeter of the original dodecagon is 72 cm, what is the side length of the scaled dodecagon?
Solution

\(Perimeter of original dodecagon = P = 72\text{ cm}\)

\(Length reducing factor = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\)

We know that a regular dodecagon has 12 equal sides, so

\(P = 12 \times L\)
\(72 = 12 \times L\)
\(\dfrac{72}{12} = L\)
\(L = 6\text{ cm}\)

Since length of original dodecagon is reduced by a factor \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\), so

\(Length of scaled dodecagon = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \times 6\)
\(= \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{2}}\)
\(= \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{2}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\)
\(= \dfrac{6\sqrt{2}}{2}\)
\(= 3\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}\)
✅ Side length of scaled dodecagon = \(3\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}\)

💡 Exam Tip:

Always match the vertices in the correct order when writing a similarity statement (e.g. \(\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF\) means \(A \leftrightarrow D\), \(B \leftrightarrow E\), \(C \leftrightarrow F\)). Most marks are lost from mismatched corresponding sides. For shadow problems, remember the ratio is height : shadow, not height : height. And always check that the ratio you set up has the same orientation on both sides — you cannot mix "side of small / side of large" with "side of large / side of small".

Two figures are similar if they have the same shape but not necessarily the same size. For polygons, this means their corresponding angles are equal and the lengths of their corresponding sides are proportional.

The most common method is the SSS (Side-Side-Side) similarity criterion: if the ratios of the lengths of the corresponding sides of two triangles are equal, then the triangles are similar. The AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) and SAS (Side-Angle-Side) criteria are also valid.
