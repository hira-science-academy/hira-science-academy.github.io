📌 Important Revision Points

  • Distance Formula: \(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\).
  • Midpoint Formula: \(M = \left( \dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)\).
  • Perimeter of a Rectangle: \(P = 2(l + w)\), where \(l\) and \(w\) are the length and width.
  • Real-life Applications: Distance between locations, midpoint of a journey, perimeter of fields, and shortest path problems.

📐 Exercise 7.3 – Questions & Solutions

Q1 If the houses of two friends are represented by coordinates \((2,6)\) and \((9,12)\) on a grid, find the straight-line distance between their houses if the grid units represent kilometres.
Solution
\(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(9 - 2)^2 + (12 - 6)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(7)^2 + (6)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{49 + 36} = \sqrt{85}\)
✅ Distance = \(\sqrt{85}\) km \(\approx 9.22\) km
Q2 Consider a straight trail (represented by coordinate plane) that starts at point \((5,7)\) and ends at point \((15,3)\). What are the coordinates of the midpoint?
Solution
\(M = \left(\dfrac{5 + 15}{2}, \dfrac{7 + 3}{2}\right)\)
\(= \left(\dfrac{20}{2}, \dfrac{10}{2}\right) = (10, 5)\)
✅ Midpoint = \((10, 5)\)
Q3 An architect is designing a park with two buildings located at \((10,8)\) and \((4,3)\) on the grid. Calculate the straight-line distance between the buildings. Assume the coordinates are in metres.
Solution
\(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(4 - 10)^2 + (3 - 8)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(-6)^2 + (-5)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{36 + 25} = \sqrt{61}\)
✅ Distance = \(\sqrt{61}\) m \(\approx 7.81\) m
Q4 A delivery driver needs to calculate the distance between two delivery locations. One location is at \((7,2)\) and the other is at \((12,10)\) on the city grid map, where each unit represents kilometres. What is the distance between the two locations?
Solution
\(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(12 - 7)^2 + (10 - 2)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(5)^2 + (8)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{25 + 64} = \sqrt{89}\)
✅ Distance = \(\sqrt{89}\) km \(\approx 9.43\) km
Q5 The start and end points of a race track are given by coordinates \((3,9)\) and \((9,13)\). What is the midpoint of the track?
Solution
\(M = \left(\dfrac{3 + 9}{2}, \dfrac{9 + 13}{2}\right)\)
\(= \left(\dfrac{12}{2}, \dfrac{22}{2}\right) = (6, 11)\)
✅ Midpoint = \((6, 11)\)
Q6 The coordinates of two points on a road are \(A(3,4)\) and \(B(7,10)\). Find the midpoint of the road.
Solution
\(M = \left(\dfrac{3 + 7}{2}, \dfrac{4 + 10}{2}\right)\)
\(= \left(\dfrac{10}{2}, \dfrac{14}{2}\right) = (5, 7)\)
✅ Midpoint = \((5, 7)\)
Q7 A ship is navigating from port A located at \((12^\circ N, 65^\circ W)\) to port B at \((20^\circ N, 45^\circ W)\). If the ship travels along the shortest path on the surface of the Earth, calculate the straight-line distance between the points.
Solution

Using the distance formula with latitude and longitude as coordinates:

\(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(20 - 12)^2 + (-45 - (-65))^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(8)^2 + (20)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{64 + 400} = \sqrt{464} = 4\sqrt{29}\)
✅ Distance \(\approx 21.54^\circ\) (in coordinate units)
Q8 Farah is fencing around a rectangular field with corners at \((0,0)\), \((0,5)\), \((8,5)\) and \((8,0)\). How much fencing material will she need to cover the entire perimeter of the field?
Solution

The length of the field is \(8 - 0 = 8\) units and the width is \(5 - 0 = 5\) units.

\(Perimeter = 2(l + w)\)
\(= 2(8 + 5)\)
\(= 2(13) = 26\) units
✅ Farah needs 26 units of fencing material.
Q9 An airplane is flying from city X at \((40^\circ N, 100^\circ W)\) to city Y at \((50^\circ N, 80^\circ W)\). Use coordinate geometry to calculate the shortest distance between these two cities.
Solution

Using the distance formula with latitude and longitude as coordinates:

\(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(50 - 40)^2 + (-80 - (-100))^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{(10)^2 + (20)^2}\)
\(= \sqrt{100 + 400} = \sqrt{500} = 10\sqrt{5}\)
✅ Distance \(\approx 22.36^\circ\) (in coordinate units)
Q10 A land surveyor is marking out a rectangular plot of land with corners at \((3,1)\), \((3,6)\), \((8,6)\), and \((8,1)\). Calculate the perimeter.
Solution

The length of the plot is \(8 - 3 = 5\) units and the width is \(6 - 1 = 5\) units.

\(Perimeter = 2(l + w)\)
\(= 2(5 + 5)\)
\(= 2(10) = 20\) units
✅ Perimeter = 20 units
Q11 A landscaper needs to install a fence around a rectangular garden. The garden has its corners at the coordinates: \(A(0,0)\), \(B(5,0)\), \(C(5,3)\), and \(D(0,3)\). How much fencing is required?
Solution

The length of the garden is \(5 - 0 = 5\) units and the width is \(3 - 0 = 3\) units.

\(Perimeter = 2(l + w)\)
\(= 2(5 + 3)\)
\(= 2(8) = 16\) units
✅ The landscaper needs 16 units of fencing.

📈 Key Concepts – Coordinate Geometry

  • Distance Formula: \(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\).
  • Midpoint Formula: \(M = \left( \dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)\).
  • Perimeter of a Rectangle: \(P = 2(l + w)\).
  • Real-life Applications: Distance between locations, midpoint of a journey, perimeter of fields, and shortest path problems.

💡 Exam Tip:

In real-life coordinate geometry problems, always identify what you are being asked to find: a distance, a midpoint, or a perimeter. Write the formula first, then substitute the given coordinates. Be careful with units — if coordinates are in kilometres, the distance will also be in kilometres. For perimeter problems, first find the side lengths using the distance formula (or simply subtract coordinates if the sides are horizontal/vertical), then apply the perimeter formula.
