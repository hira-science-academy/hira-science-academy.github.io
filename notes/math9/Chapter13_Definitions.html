  • Range: \(0 \leq P(A) \leq 1\).
  • Complement: \(P(A') = 1 - P(A)\).
  • Sum of an event and its complement: \(P(A) + P(A') = 1\).

📒 Chapter 13 – Definitions & Formulas

D1 Define probability.

Probability is the chance of a particular event occurring. It is calculated as:

\(P(A) = \dfrac{n(A)}{n(S)}\)

where \(P(A)\) is the probability of event \(A\), \(n(A)\) is the number of favourable outcomes, and \(n(S)\) is the total number of possible outcomes.

D2 Where does the word probability come from?

The term "probability" originates from the Latin word "Probabilitas", meaning "probity". The Italian physician and mathematician Girolamo Cardano is regarded as the father of probability theory.

D3 Define experiment.

The process which generates results is called an experiment. For example, tossing a coin, rolling a dice, etc.

D4 Define outcomes.

The results of an experiment are called outcomes. For example, the possible outcomes of tossing a coin are head or tail; the possible outcomes of rolling a dice are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6.

D5 Define favourable outcome.

A favourable outcome represents how many times we expect the things to happen. For example, while tossing a coin there is 1 favourable outcome of getting a head (or a tail); while rolling a dice, there are 3 favourable outcomes of getting multiples of 2, i.e. \(\{2, 4, 6\}\).

D6 Define sample space.

The set of all possible outcomes of an experiment is called the sample space. It is denoted by \(S\). For example, when tossing a coin, \(S = \{H, T\}\); when rolling a dice, \(S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}\).

D7 Define event.

The set of results of an experiment is called an event. For example, while rolling a dice, getting an even number is an event:

\(A = \{2, 4, 6\},\quad n(A) = 3\)

meaning there are 3 favourable outcomes.

D8 What are the different types of events in probability?
  1. Certain event: an event which is sure to occur. Its probability is \(1\) (or \(100\%\)).
  2. Impossible event: an event that cannot occur in any trial. Its probability is \(0\) (or \(0\%\)).
  3. Likely event: an event that will probably occur. It has a greater chance, around \(75\%\).
  4. Unlikely event: an event that will probably not occur. It has a lesser chance, around \(25\%\).
  5. Equally likely events: events that have equal chances of occurring. Their probability is \(0.5\) (or \(50\%\)).

Note: The range of probability for any event is \(0 \leq P(A) \leq 1\).

D9 What is the probability of an event not occurring (complement of an event)?

The probability of an event not occurring is called the complement of the event. It is written as \(P(A')\) or \(P(A^c)\) and is calculated using:

\(P(A') = 1 - P(A)\)

Example: When tossing a coin, let \(A\) be the event of getting a head, so \(P(A) = \dfrac{1}{2}\). The event of not getting a head (i.e. getting a tail) is the complement, \(A'\).

\(P(A') = 1 - P(A) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}\)

Note: The sum of the probability of an event and its complement is always \(P(A) + P(A') = 1\). For example, if the probability of rain today is 0.3, the probability of no rain is \(1 - 0.3 = 0.7\).

D10 What is a deck of cards?

A deck of cards has 52 cards in total. There are 4 suits in the deck:

SuitColour
Spades (♠)Black
Clubs (♣)Black
Hearts (♥)Red
Diamonds (♦)Red

Each suit has 13 cards.

D11 What cards are in each suit?

Each suit has the same set of 13 cards:

  • 1 Ace
  • 9 number cards (from 2 to 10)
  • 3 face cards: 1 Jack, 1 Queen, 1 King

So total per suit \(= 1\) (Ace) \(+ 9\) (numbers) \(+ 3\) (face cards) \(= 13\) cards.

Red cards vs Black cards: Red cards \(=\) Hearts \(+\) Diamonds \(= 26\) cards; Black cards \(=\) Spades \(+\) Clubs \(= 26\) cards.

Important points to remember:

  • Total cards \(= 52\)
  • Each suit \(= 13\) cards
  • Total aces \(= 4\); total kings \(= 4\); total queens \(= 4\); total jacks \(= 4\)
  • Total face cards \(= 12\) (3 from each suit)
  • Red cards \(= 26\); black cards \(= 26\)
D12 Define relative frequency.

Relative frequency shows how often a specific event happens compared to the total number of events or trials. It is calculated using:

\(\text{Relative Frequency} = \dfrac{\text{Frequency of specific event}}{\text{Total frequency}} = \dfrac{x}{N}\)

where \(x\) = frequency of the specific event and \(N = \sum f\) = total of all frequencies.

Notes: (i) The sum of all relative frequencies is always equal to or approximately equal to 1. (ii) Relative frequency is an estimated probability of an event occurring when an experiment is repeated a fixed number of times.

D13 What is expected frequency?

Expected frequency is a measure that estimates how often an event should occur, based on its probability. It is calculated using:

\(\text{Expected frequency} = \text{Total number of trials} \times \text{Probability of the event} = N \times P(A)\)

Note: The sum of all expected frequencies is always equal to or approximately equal to the fixed number of trials.

💡 Exam Tip:

Learn each definition together with its formula — the definitions section is worth several marks in every board paper, and the formulas are tested in the exercises. For the deck of cards, memorise the four suits, the 13-per-suit structure, and the fact that there are 26 red and 26 black cards, 4 of each rank and 12 face cards total. Remember the two frequency formulas: relative frequency is \(\dfrac{x}{N}\) (measured after the experiment), while expected frequency is \(N \times P(A)\) (predicted before it). If an exam asks you to define a term, copy the textbook wording closely and add one worked example to make it concrete.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions – Chapter 13 Definitions

Probability is the chance of a particular event occurring. It is calculated as \(P(A) = \dfrac{n(A)}{n(S)}\), where \(n(A)\) is the number of favourable outcomes and \(n(S)\) is the total number of possible outcomes.

The sample space is the set of all possible outcomes of an experiment. It is denoted by \(S\). When tossing a coin, \(S = \{H, T\}\). When rolling a die, \(S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}\).

The types of events are: certain event (probability 1 or 100%), impossible event (probability 0 or 0%), likely event (around 75%), unlikely event (around 25%), and equally likely events (probability 0.5 or 50%). The range of probability for any event is \(0 \leq P(A) \leq 1\).

A standard deck has 52 cards: 4 suits (spades and clubs are black, hearts and diamonds are red), with 13 cards per suit. There are 4 aces, 4 kings, 4 queens and 4 jacks, giving 12 face cards total. There are 26 red cards and 26 black cards.

Relative frequency \(= \dfrac{\text{frequency of specific event}}{\text{total frequency}} = \dfrac{x}{N}\), where \(x\) is the frequency of the specific event and \(N\) is the sum of all frequencies. Expected frequency \(= N \times P(A)\). The sum of all relative frequencies in a table is always \(1\).
