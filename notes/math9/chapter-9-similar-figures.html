⚡ Key Concepts – Similar Figures

Similar Polygons
\(A \sim B\)

Two polygons are similar if their corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are proportional.

Ratio of Areas
\(\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\frac{l_1}{l_2}\right)^2\)

The ratio of the areas of similar figures is the square of the ratio of their corresponding lengths.

Ratio of Volumes
\(\frac{V_1}{V_2} = \left(\frac{l_1}{l_2}\right)^3\)

The ratio of the volumes of similar solids is the cube of the ratio of their corresponding lengths.

Interior Angle of Polygon
\(\frac{(n-2) \times 180^{\circ}}{n}\)

The formula to find the measure of each interior angle of a regular \(n\)-sided polygon.

Exterior Angle of Polygon
\(\frac{360^{\circ}}{n}\)

The formula to find the measure of each exterior angle of a regular \(n\)-sided polygon.

Tessellation
Sum of angles = \(360^{\circ}\)

A pattern of shapes that fit together perfectly without gaps. Only regular triangles, squares, and hexagons can tessellate.

❓ Chapter 9 Frequently Asked Questions

What are similar figures?

Similar figures have the same shape but not necessarily the same size. For polygons to be similar, their corresponding angles must be equal, and their corresponding sides must be proportional.

What is the relationship between the area and volume of similar figures?

If the ratio of corresponding lengths of two similar figures is \(a:b\), then the ratio of their areas is \(a^2:b^2\), and the ratio of their volumes (if they are solids) is \(a^3:b^3\).

What is a regular polygon?

A regular polygon is a polygon that has all sides equal in length and all interior angles equal in measure. Examples include equilateral triangles, squares, and regular hexagons.

What is tessellation?

Tessellation is a pattern of shapes that fit together perfectly to cover a flat surface without any gaps or overlaps. Regular polygons like triangles, squares, and hexagons can tessellate.

How is the mass of a similar solid related to its volume?

For solids made of the same material, mass is directly proportional to volume. Therefore, the ratio of their masses is equal to the ratio of their volumes, which is the cube of the ratio of their corresponding lengths.
