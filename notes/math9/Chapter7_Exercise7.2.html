📌 Important Revision Points

  • Slope Formula: \(m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\).
  • Inclination: \(\tan \alpha = m\).
  • Collinear Points: Slopes between any two pairs of points are equal.
  • Parallel Lines: \(m_1 = m_2\).
  • Perpendicular Lines: \(m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1\).
  • Equation of a Line: Point-slope form: \(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\).
  • Slope-Intercept Form: \(y = mx + c\).
  • Two-Intercept Form: \(\dfrac{x}{a} + \dfrac{y}{b} = 1\).
  • Normal Form: \(x \cos \alpha + y \sin \alpha = p\).

📐 Exercise 7.2 – Questions & Solutions

Q1 Find the slope and inclination of the line joining the points:
(i) \((-2,4)\); \((5,11)\)
(ii) \((3,-2)\); \((2,7)\)
(iii) \((4,6)\); \((4,8)\)
Solution

(i) \((-2,4)\) and \((5,11)\)

\(Slope = m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\)
\(m = \dfrac{11 - 4}{5 - (-2)} = \dfrac{7}{7} = 1\)

By using the formula of inclination:

\(\tan \alpha = m \Rightarrow \tan \alpha = 1 \Rightarrow \alpha = \tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ\)

(ii) \((3,-2)\) and \((2,7)\)

\(Slope = m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\)
\(m = \dfrac{7 - (-2)}{2 - 3} = \dfrac{9}{-1} = -9\)

By using the formula of inclination:

\(\tan \alpha = m \Rightarrow \tan \alpha = -9\)
\(-\tan \alpha = 9 \Rightarrow \tan(180^\circ - \alpha) = 9\)
\(180^\circ - \alpha = \tan^{-1}(9) = 83.66^\circ\)
\(\alpha = 180^\circ - 83.66^\circ = 96.34^\circ\)

(iii) \((4,6)\) and \((4,8)\)

\(Slope = m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\)
\(m = \dfrac{8 - 6}{4 - 4} = \dfrac{2}{0} = \infty\)

The line is vertical, so its inclination is \(90^\circ\).

✅ (i) \(m = 1\), \(\alpha = 45^\circ\)  |  (ii) \(m = -9\), \(\alpha = 96.34^\circ\)  |  (iii) \(m = \infty\), \(\alpha = 90^\circ\)
Q2 By means of slopes, show that the following points lie on the same line:
(i) \(A(-1,-3)\), \(B(1,5)\), \(C(2,9)\)
(ii) \(P(4,-5)\), \(Q(7,5)\), \(R(10,15)\)
Solution

(i) \(A(-1,-3)\), \(B(1,5)\), \(C(2,9)\)

\(Slope\ of\ AB = m_1 = \dfrac{5 - (-3)}{1 - (-1)} = \dfrac{8}{2} = 4\)
\(Slope\ of\ BC = m_2 = \dfrac{9 - 5}{2 - 1} = \dfrac{4}{1} = 4\)

As \(m_1 = m_2\), hence \(A, B, C\) are collinear.

(ii) \(P(4,-5)\), \(Q(7,5)\), \(R(10,15)\)

\(Slope\ of\ PQ = m_1 = \dfrac{5 - (-5)}{7 - 4} = \dfrac{10}{3}\)
\(Slope\ of\ QR = m_2 = \dfrac{15 - 5}{10 - 7} = \dfrac{10}{3}\)

As \(m_1 = m_2\), hence \(P, Q, R\) are collinear.

✅ Both sets of points are collinear.
Q3 Find \(k\) so that the line joining \(A(7,3)\), \(B(k,-6)\) and the line joining \(C(-4,5)\), \(D(-6,4)\) are:
(i) parallel
(ii) perpendicular
Solution
\(Slope\ of\ AB = m_1 = \dfrac{-6 - 3}{k - 7} = \dfrac{-9}{k - 7}\)
\(Slope\ of\ CD = m_2 = \dfrac{4 - 5}{-6 - (-4)} = \dfrac{-1}{-2} = \dfrac{1}{2}\)

(i) For parallel lines: \(m_1 = m_2\)

\(\dfrac{-9}{k - 7} = \dfrac{1}{2}\)
\(-18 = k - 7 \Rightarrow k = -11\)

(ii) For perpendicular lines: \(m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1\)

\(\left(\dfrac{-9}{k - 7}\right) \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = -1\)
\(\dfrac{-9}{2(k - 7)} = -1\)
\(-9 = -2(k - 7)\)
\(-9 = -2k + 14\)
\(2k = 23 \Rightarrow k = \dfrac{23}{2}\)
✅ (i) \(k = -11\)    (ii) \(k = \frac{23}{2}\)
Q4 Using slopes, show that the triangle with vertices \(A(6,1)\), \(B(2,7)\) and \(C(-6,-7)\) is a right triangle.
Solution
\(Slope\ of\ AB = m_1 = \dfrac{7 - 1}{2 - 6} = \dfrac{6}{-4} = -\dfrac{3}{2}\)
\(Slope\ of\ BC = m_2 = \dfrac{-7 - 7}{-6 - 2} = \dfrac{-14}{-8} = \dfrac{7}{4}\)
\(Slope\ of\ AC = m_3 = \dfrac{-7 - 1}{-6 - 6} = \dfrac{-8}{-12} = \dfrac{2}{3}\)

Now:

\(m_1 \cdot m_3 = \left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right) = -1\)

So, side \(AB \perp AC\). Hence \(A, B, C\) are vertices of a right triangle.

✅ \(AB \perp AC\), so \(\triangle ABC\) is a right triangle.
Q5 Two pairs of points are given. Find whether the two lines determined by these points are:
(i) parallel
(ii) perpendicular
(iii) none
(a) \((1,-2), (2,4)\) and \((4,1), (-8,2)\)
(b) \((-3,4), (6,2)\) and \((4,5), (-2,-7)\)
Solution

(a) \(A(1,-2)\), \(B(2,4)\) and \(C(4,1)\), \(D(-8,2)\)

\(Slope\ of\ first\ line = m_1 = \dfrac{4 - (-2)}{2 - 1} = \dfrac{6}{1} = 6\)
\(Slope\ of\ second\ line = m_2 = \dfrac{2 - 1}{-8 - 4} = \dfrac{1}{-12} = -\dfrac{1}{12}\)

Since \(m_1 \neq m_2\) and \(m_1 \cdot m_2 = 6 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{12}\right) = -\frac{1}{2} \neq -1\), the lines are neither parallel nor perpendicular.

(b) \(A(-3,4)\), \(B(6,2)\) and \(C(4,5)\), \(D(-2,-7)\)

\(Slope\ of\ first\ line = m_1 = \dfrac{2 - 4}{6 - (-3)} = \dfrac{-2}{9}\)
\(Slope\ of\ second\ line = m_2 = \dfrac{-7 - 5}{-2 - 4} = \dfrac{-12}{-6} = 2\)

Since \(m_1 \neq m_2\) and \(m_1 \cdot m_2 = \left(-\frac{2}{9}\right) \cdot 2 = -\frac{4}{9} \neq -1\), the lines are neither parallel nor perpendicular.

✅ (a) Neither    (b) Neither
Q6 Find an equation of the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining the points \(A(3,5)\) and \(B(9,8)\).
Solution
\(Slope\ of\ AB = m_1 = \dfrac{8 - 5}{9 - 3} = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}\)
\(Slope\ of\ \perp\ bisector = m_2 = \dfrac{-1}{m_1} = \dfrac{-1}{1/2} = -2\)

Let \(M\) be the midpoint of \(AB\):

\(M = \left(\dfrac{3 + 9}{2}, \dfrac{5 + 8}{2}\right) = \left(6, \dfrac{13}{2}\right)\)

Equation of perpendicular bisector passing through \((6, \frac{13}{2})\) with slope \(-2\):

\(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\)
\(y - \dfrac{13}{2} = -2(x - 6)\)
\(2y - 13 = -4x + 24\)
\(4x + 2y - 37 = 0\)
✅ \(4x + 2y - 37 = 0\)
Q7 Find an equation of the line through \((-4,-6)\) and perpendicular to a line having slope \(\frac{-3}{2}\).
Solution
\(Slope\ of\ given\ line = m_1 = -\dfrac{3}{2}\)
\(Slope\ of\ required\ \perp\ line = m_2 = \dfrac{-1}{m_1} = \dfrac{-1}{-3/2} = \dfrac{2}{3}\)

Equation of line passing through \((-4,-6)\) with slope \(\frac{2}{3}\):

\(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\)
\(y - (-6) = \dfrac{2}{3}(x - (-4))\)
\(3(y + 6) = 2(x + 4)\)
\(3y + 18 = 2x + 8\)
\(2x - 3y - 10 = 0\)
✅ \(2x - 3y - 10 = 0\)
Q8 Find an equation of the line through \((11,-5)\) and parallel to a line with slope \(-24\).
Solution
\(Slope\ of\ given\ line = m_1 = -24\)
\(Slope\ of\ required\ parallel\ line = m_2 = -24\)

Equation of line passing through \((11,-5)\) with slope \(-24\):

\(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\)
\(y - (-5) = -24(x - 11)\)
\(y + 5 = -24x + 264\)
\(24x + y - 259 = 0\)
✅ \(24x + y - 259 = 0\)
Q9 Convert each of the following equations into slope-intercept form, two-intercept form, and normal form:
(a) \(2x - 4y + 11 = 0\)
(b) \(4x + 7y - 2 = 0\)
(c) \(15y - 8x + 3 = 0\)
Solution

(a) \(2x - 4y + 11 = 0\)

Slope-intercept form:

\(4y = 2x + 11 \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{11}{4}\)

Two-intercept form:

\(2x - 4y = -11 \Rightarrow \dfrac{x}{-11/2} + \dfrac{y}{11/4} = 1\)

Normal form:

\(\dfrac{2x}{-2\sqrt{5}} - \dfrac{4y}{-2\sqrt{5}} = \dfrac{-11}{-2\sqrt{5}}\)
\(\Rightarrow -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}x + \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}y = \dfrac{11}{2\sqrt{5}}\)
\(\cos \alpha = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\), \(\sin \alpha = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\), \(p = \dfrac{11}{2\sqrt{5}}\)
\(\alpha = 116.56^\circ\)
\(x \cos 116.56^\circ + y \sin 116.56^\circ = \dfrac{11}{2\sqrt{5}}\)

(b) \(4x + 7y - 2 = 0\)

Slope-intercept form:

\(7y = -4x + 2 \Rightarrow y = -\dfrac{4}{7}x + \dfrac{2}{7}\)

Two-intercept form:

\(4x + 7y = 2 \Rightarrow \dfrac{x}{1/2} + \dfrac{y}{2/7} = 1\)

Normal form:

\(\dfrac{4x}{\sqrt{65}} + \dfrac{7y}{\sqrt{65}} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{65}}\)
\(\cos \alpha = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{65}}\), \(\sin \alpha = \dfrac{7}{\sqrt{65}}\), \(p = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{65}}\)
\(\alpha = 60.26^\circ\)
\(x \cos 60.26^\circ + y \sin 60.26^\circ = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{65}}\)

(c) \(15y - 8x + 3 = 0\)

Slope-intercept form:

\(15y = 8x - 3 \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{8}{15}x - \dfrac{1}{5}\)

Two-intercept form:

\(-8x + 15y = -3 \Rightarrow \dfrac{x}{3/8} + \dfrac{y}{-1/5} = 1\)

Normal form:

\(\dfrac{-8x}{-17} + \dfrac{15y}{-17} = \dfrac{-3}{-17}\)
\(\dfrac{8}{17}x - \dfrac{15}{17}y = \dfrac{3}{17}\)
\(\cos \alpha = \dfrac{8}{17}\), \(\sin \alpha = -\dfrac{15}{17}\), \(p = \dfrac{3}{17}\)
\(\alpha = 360^\circ - 61.93^\circ = 298.07^\circ\)
\(x \cos 298.07^\circ + y \sin 298.07^\circ = \dfrac{3}{17}\)
Q10 In each of the following check whether the two lines are (i) parallel (ii) perpendicular (iii) neither parallel nor perpendicular:
(a) \(2x + y - 3 = 0\); \(4x + 2y + 5 = 0\)
(b) \(3y = 2x + 5\); \(3x + 2y - 8 = 0\)
(c) \(4y + 2x - 1 = 0\); \(x - 2y - 7 = 0\)
Solution

(a) \(2x + y - 3 = 0\); \(4x + 2y + 5 = 0\)

\(l_1: 2x + y - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow y = -2x + 3 \Rightarrow m_1 = -2\)
\(l_2: 4x + 2y + 5 = 0 \Rightarrow 2y = -4x - 5 \Rightarrow y = -2x - \frac{5}{2} \Rightarrow m_2 = -2\)

Since \(m_1 = m_2 = -2\), the lines are parallel.

(b) \(3y = 2x + 5\); \(3x + 2y - 8 = 0\)

\(l_1: 3y = 2x + 5 \Rightarrow y = \frac{2}{3}x + \frac{5}{3} \Rightarrow m_1 = \frac{2}{3}\)
\(l_2: 3x + 2y - 8 = 0 \Rightarrow 2y = -3x + 8 \Rightarrow y = -\frac{3}{2}x + 4 \Rightarrow m_2 = -\frac{3}{2}\)

Since \(m_1 \cdot m_2 = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)\left(-\frac{3}{2}\right) = -1\), the lines are perpendicular.

(c) \(4y + 2x - 1 = 0\); \(x - 2y - 7 = 0\)

\(l_1: 4y + 2x - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow 4y = -2x + 1 \Rightarrow y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{1}{4} \Rightarrow m_1 = -\frac{1}{2}\)
\(l_2: x - 2y - 7 = 0 \Rightarrow 2y = x - 7 \Rightarrow y = \frac{1}{2}x - \frac{7}{2} \Rightarrow m_2 = \frac{1}{2}\)

Since \(m_1 \neq m_2\) and \(m_1 \cdot m_2 = \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = -\frac{1}{4} \neq -1\), the lines are neither parallel nor perpendicular.

✅ (a) Parallel    (b) Perpendicular    (c) Neither

📈 Key Concepts – Coordinate Geometry

  • Slope Formula: \(m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\).
  • Inclination: \(\tan \alpha = m\).
  • Collinear Points: Slopes between any two pairs of points are equal.
  • Parallel Lines: \(m_1 = m_2\).
  • Perpendicular Lines: \(m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1\).
  • Equation of a Line: Point-slope form: \(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\).
  • Slope-Intercept Form: \(y = mx + c\).
  • Two-Intercept Form: \(\dfrac{x}{a} + \dfrac{y}{b} = 1\).
  • Normal Form: \(x \cos \alpha + y \sin \alpha = p\).

💡 Exam Tip:

For slope and inclination problems, always write the formula first, then substitute the coordinates carefully. When proving collinearity, calculate the slopes between consecutive points and show they are equal. For parallel/perpendicular lines, use the slope conditions \(m_1 = m_2\) and \(m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1\) respectively. When finding equations of lines, choose the most convenient form based on the given information (point-slope for a point and slope, two-intercept for intercepts, normal form for angle and distance). Be careful with signs, especially when subtracting negative coordinates.

The slope formula is \(m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\), which calculates the steepness of a line passing through two points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\).

The inclination \(\alpha\) is found using the formula \(\tan \alpha = m\), where \(m\) is the slope of the line. The angle is measured counterclockwise from the positive x-axis.

Three points are collinear if the slopes between any two pairs of points are equal. For points A, B, and C, calculate the slope of AB and the slope of BC. If \(m_{AB} = m_{BC}\), then the points are collinear.
