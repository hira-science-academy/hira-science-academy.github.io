📌 Formula Summary

  • Probability of an event: \(P(A) = \dfrac{n(A)}{n(S)}\).
  • Complementary rule: \(P(A') = 1 - P(A)\).
  • Relative frequency: \(\text{R.F.} = \dfrac{x}{N}\) where \(x\) is the frequency of the specific event and \(N = \sum f\).
  • Expected frequency: \(E = N \times P(A)\).
  • Probability range: \(0 \leq P(A) \leq 1\).
  • Sum of all relative frequencies in a table = 1.

📐 Review Exercise 13 – Questions & Solutions

Q2 Define the following:
(i) relative frequency
(ii) expected frequency
Solution

(i) Relative frequency

Relative frequency shows how often a specific event happens compared to the total number of events or trials. It is calculated using:

\(\text{Relative Frequency} = \dfrac{\text{Frequency of specific event}}{\text{Total frequency}} = \dfrac{x}{N}\)

where \(x\) is the frequency of the specific event and \(N = \sum f\) is the total of all frequencies.

(ii) Expected frequency

Expected frequency is a measure that estimates how often an event should occur, based on its probability. It is calculated using:

\(\text{Expected frequency} = \text{Total number of trials} \times \text{Probability of the event} = N \times P(A)\)
✅ Both definitions stated with their formulas.
Q3 An urn contains 10 red balls, 5 green balls and 8 blue balls. Find the probability of selecting at random:
(i) a green ball
(ii) a red ball
(iii) a blue ball
(iv) not a red ball
(v) not a green ball
Solution

Total number of balls in the urn:

\(n(S) = 10 + 5 + 8 = 23\)

(i) A green ball

\(n(A) = 5,\quad P(A) = \dfrac{5}{23}\)

(ii) A red ball

\(n(B) = 10,\quad P(B) = \dfrac{10}{23}\)

(iii) A blue ball

\(n(C) = 8,\quad P(C) = \dfrac{8}{23}\)

(iv) Not a red ball

\(P(B') = 1 - P(B) = 1 - \dfrac{10}{23} = \dfrac{13}{23}\)

(v) Not a green ball

\(P(A') = 1 - P(A) = 1 - \dfrac{5}{23} = \dfrac{18}{23}\)
✅ (i) 5/23    (ii) 10/23    (iii) 8/23    (iv) 13/23    (v) 18/23
Q4 Three coins are tossed together. Find the probability of getting:
(i) exactly three heads
(ii) at least two tails
(iii) not at least two heads
(iv) not exactly two heads
Solution

The sample space for three coin tosses is:

\(S = \{HHH, HHT, HTH, THH, HTT, THT, TTH, TTT\},\quad n(S) = 8\)

(i) Exactly three heads

\(A = \{HHH\},\quad n(A) = 1,\quad P(A) = \dfrac{1}{8}\)

(ii) At least two tails

\(B = \{HTT, THT, TTH, TTT\},\quad n(B) = 4,\quad P(B) = \dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{1}{2}\)

(iii) Not at least two heads

"At least two heads" is \(C = \{HHT, HTH, THH, HHH\}\) with \(n(C) = 4\). So:

\(P(C) = \dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{1}{2},\quad P(C') = 1 - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}\)

(iv) Not exactly two heads

"Exactly two heads" is \(D = \{HHT, HTH, THH\}\) with \(n(D) = 3\). So:

\(P(D) = \dfrac{3}{8},\quad P(D') = 1 - \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{5}{8}\)
✅ (i) 1/8    (ii) 1/2    (iii) 1/2    (iv) 5/8
Q5 A card is drawn from a well-shuffled pack of 52 playing cards. What is the probability of getting:
(i) king or jack of red colour
(ii) not "2" of club and spade
Solution

Total number of cards \(= 52\), so \(n(S) = 52\).

(i) King or jack of red colour

Red suits are Hearts and Diamonds. Each red suit has 1 king and 1 jack, so:

\(n(A) = 2 \text{ red kings} + 2 \text{ red jacks} = 4\)
\(P(A) = \dfrac{4}{52} = \dfrac{1}{13}\)

(ii) Not "2" of club and spade

There are exactly two cards that are "2" of club and "2" of spade:

\(n(B) = 2,\quad P(B) = \dfrac{2}{52} = \dfrac{1}{26}\)
\(P(B') = 1 - \dfrac{1}{26} = \dfrac{25}{26}\)
✅ (i) 1/13    (ii) 25/26
Q6 Six coins are tossed 600 times. The number of occurrences of tails is recorded in the table. Find the relative frequency.
No. of tails: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6
Frequency: 110, 90, 105, 80, 76, 123, 16
Solution

Total number of trials \(N = 110 + 90 + 105 + 80 + 76 + 123 + 16 = 600\).

Relative frequency \(= \dfrac{f}{600}\):

Q7 From a lot containing 25 items, 8 items are defective. Find the relative frequency of non-defective items, and also find the expected frequency of non-defective items.
Solution

Total number of items \(= 25\), so \(n(S) = 25\).

Let \(A\) be the event of getting a defective item:

\(n(A) = 8,\quad P(A) = \dfrac{8}{25}\)

Probability (and relative frequency) of non-defective items

\(P(A') = 1 - P(A) = 1 - \dfrac{8}{25} = \dfrac{17}{25} = 0.68\)

Expected frequency of non-defective items

With \(N = 25\) items:

\(E = N \times P(A') = 25 \times \dfrac{17}{25} = 17\)
✅ Relative frequency of non-defective items = \(17/25 = 0.68\); expected frequency = \(17\)

💡 Exam Tip:

In a review exercise the marks come from showing the method, not from the final number. Always write the sample space first — \(n(S) = 8\) for three coins, \(n(S) = 52\) for a deck of cards, \(n(S) = \text{total balls}\) for urn problems. For complementary questions, compute \(P(A)\) first and then apply \(P(A') = 1 - P(A)\) — it saves time. For relative frequency, remember the answer must be between 0 and 1 and all the values must sum to 1; for expected frequency, use \(E = N \times P(A)\) — the number of trials times the probability. If the probability is already given (e.g. 17/25 non-defective), just multiply by the number of trials; if the probability needs to be found first (as in Q7), compute the complement of the defective probability.

Relative frequency = (frequency of the specific event) / (total frequency). If \(x\) is the frequency of the specific event and \(N = \sum f\) is the total of all frequencies, then \(\text{Relative Frequency} = \dfrac{x}{N}\). The sum of all relative frequencies in a table is always \(1\).

Expected frequency = total number of trials × probability of the event, i.e. \(E = N \times P(A)\). For example, if there are 25 items and 8 are defective, the probability of a non-defective item is \(17/25\), so in one trial of 25 items the expected frequency of non-defective items is \(25 \times 17/25 = 17\).
