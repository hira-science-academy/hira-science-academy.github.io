📖 Chapter 4 Definitions – Factorization

This chapter covers fundamental concepts of factorization, including classification of expressions, HCF and LCM, and square roots of algebraic expressions.

Chapter 4 provides comprehensive definitions and explanations of factorization, HCF, LCM, and their applications. Understanding these concepts is crucial for building a strong foundation in algebra.

The chapter covers factorization techniques, different methods for finding HCF and LCM, the relationship between them, and methods for finding square roots of algebraic expressions.

Key Concepts Covered

  • Definition and importance of factorization
  • Classification of algebraic expressions (monomial, binomial, trinomial, polynomial)
  • Highest Common Factor (HCF) and its methods
  • Least Common Multiple (LCM) and its methods
  • Relationship between LCM and HCF
  • Square roots of algebraic expressions

Important Note:

Understanding these definitions is essential for solving the exercises and problems in this chapter. Review them regularly and practice applying them in different contexts.

📐 Detailed Definitions

Def 1 Define factorization.
Definition

The process of expressing an algebraic expression in terms of its factors is called factorization.

Example: \(5a + 5b = 5(a + b)\)

Def 2 How do we classify expressions based on the number of terms?
Definition

We use different terms to express the number of terms in an expression:

  • Monomial: An expression with only one term, such as \(2x\) or \(3y\).
  • Binomial: An expression with two terms, such as \(2x + 3y\) or \(x^2 - 4\).
  • Trinomial: An expression with three terms, such as \(x^2 + 4x - 3\) or \(2x^2 - 5x + 1\).
  • Polynomial: A broad category that includes all types of expressions with one or more terms.
Def 3 Define HCF (Highest Common Factor) and how is it found?
Definition

The HCF of two or more algebraic expressions refers to the greatest algebraic expression which divides them without leaving a remainder.

We can find the HCF of given expressions by the following two methods:

  • (i) By factorization
  • (ii) By division
Def 4 Define LCM (Least Common Multiple) and how is it found?
Definition

The LCM of two or more algebraic expressions is the smallest expression that is divisible by each of the given expressions.

To find the LCM by factorization, we use the formula:

\(\text{LCM} = \text{Common factors} \times \text{Non-common factors}\)
Def 5 Explain the relationship between LCM and HCF.
Definition

The relationship between LCM and HCF can be expressed as follows:

\(\text{LCM} \times \text{HCF} = p(x) \times q(x)\)

Where,

  • \(p(x) = 1^{\text{st}}\) polynomial
  • \(q(x) = 2^{\text{nd}}\) polynomial
Def 6 Define square root.
Definition

The square root of an algebraic expression refers to a value that, when multiplied by itself, gives the original expression.

Example: The square root of \(4a^2\) is \(\pm 2a\), because \((2a) \times (2a) = 4a^2\) and \((-2a) \times (-2a) = 4a^2\).

There are following two methods for finding the square root of an algebraic expression:

  • (i) By factorization method
  • (ii) By division method

💡 Exam Tip:

For definition-based questions, always write the formal definition clearly and then provide an example to illustrate the concept. For HCF and LCM problems, remember that the product of HCF and LCM equals the product of the two polynomials. For square root questions, understand both factorization and division methods. Memorize these definitions as they form the foundation for solving numerical problems.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions – Chapter 4 Definitions

Factorization is the process of expressing an algebraic expression as a product of its factors. For example, \(5a + 5b = 5(a + b)\). It is the reverse of expansion.

Expressions are classified as:

  • Monomial: One term (e.g., \(2x\))
  • Binomial: Two terms (e.g., \(2x + 3y\))
  • Trinomial: Three terms (e.g., \(x^2 + 4x - 3\))
  • Polynomial: A broad category including all expressions with one or more terms

The HCF (Highest Common Factor) of two or more algebraic expressions is the greatest algebraic expression that divides them without leaving a remainder. It can be found by factorization or division method.

The LCM (Least Common Multiple) of two or more algebraic expressions is the smallest expression that is divisible by each of the given expressions. It is found by taking the product of common factors and non-common factors.

For any two polynomials \(p(x)\) and \(q(x)\), the product of their LCM and HCF equals the product of the polynomials:

\(\text{LCM} \times \text{HCF} = p(x) \times q(x)\)

The square root of an algebraic expression is a value that, when multiplied by itself, gives the original expression. For example, the square root of \(4a^2\) is \(\pm 2a\). It can be found by factorization or division method.
