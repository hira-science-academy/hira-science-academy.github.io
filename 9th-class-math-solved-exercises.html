Why Working Through Solved Exercises Builds Real Math Skill

Reading about a method and being able to apply it are two very different skills. The fastest way to close that gap in Class 9 Mathematics is to work through solved exercises — attempting each question yourself, then checking your working, line by line, against a fully solved version. This page brings together every exercise from all 13 chapters of the PECTAA 2026 syllabus in exactly that format.

Each solution is broken into clear, numbered steps: the formula being used, the substitution of values, and the simplification to a final answer. This matters because Punjab Board examiners award marks for correct method, not just a correct final number — so seeing the full working helps you understand what a complete, mark-scoring answer actually looks like, not just what the answer is.

Alongside the standard exercises, every chapter's review exercise is solved as well. Review exercises tend to mix questions from across the chapter, and sometimes from earlier chapters too, which makes them a useful checkpoint for whether you've genuinely understood a topic or just memorized one type of question.

Chapters like Trigonometry, Coordinate Geometry, and Logarithms are where students most often get stuck partway through a solution — not because the concept is unclear, but because one intermediate step doesn't match what they expected. Having the full working available means you can pinpoint exactly where your approach diverged, rather than guessing at what went wrong.

For self-study, this resource works best as a two-step habit: attempt the exercise on your own first, then open the solution to compare. Simply reading a solved exercise without attempting it first gives a false sense of understanding — the real learning happens in the gap between your attempt and the correct method.

These solved exercises are useful for teachers setting homework and grading student work consistently, and for parents checking a child's exercise book without having to re-derive the mathematics from scratch. Since the exercise numbering follows the same PECTAA 2026 syllabus used by all 10 BISE boards in Punjab — Bahawalpur, DG Khan, Faisalabad, Federal, Gujranwala, Lahore, Multan, Rawalpindi, Sahiwal, and Sargodha — this page applies no matter which board a student is registered under.

Open any chapter below to see its full list of solved exercises. If you're looking to test yourself rather than check your working, our dedicated MCQs and important-questions pages are linked further down this page.

💡 Practice Tips for Solving Exercises

  • • Attempt before checking: Always try a question yourself before opening its solution.
  • • Work in order: Solve exercises in sequence, since later questions often build on earlier ones.
  • • Write every step: Board examiners award marks for method, so practice showing full working, not shortcuts.
  • • Don't skip review exercises: They mix question types and reveal gaps a single exercise might hide.
  • • Time yourself occasionally: Solving a full exercise under a time limit builds real exam speed.

🎯 Turning Practice Into Exam Marks

  • • Find your divergence point: Compare your working line by line to locate exactly where an error started.
  • • Redo the corrected version: Rewrite the full solution yourself once you understand the mistake.
  • • Track recurring errors: If a mistake repeats across exercises, revisit that chapter's definitions.
  • • Test yourself afterward: Use the MCQs test above to confirm you understood the concept, not just the steps.
  • • Revisit weak exercises near exams: Re-solve any exercise you struggled with in the week before your test.

⚠️ Mistakes These Solutions Help You Catch

  • • Skipping steps: Comparing full working shows exactly which step earns marks and which you're missing.
  • • Formula misuse: Especially common in Trigonometry, Logarithms, and Coordinate Geometry exercises.
  • • Misreading the question: Re-read the problem after seeing the solution to spot what you overlooked.
  • • Sign errors: A dropped negative sign is easy to catch when comparing against a full solution.

🧠 Problem-Solving Habits Worth Building

  • • Break it down: Divide complex problems into smaller, manageable steps, as shown in each solution.
  • • Visualize: Draw diagrams or graphs for geometry and coordinate problems before solving.
  • • Verify your answer: Plug your final answer back into the original equation to double-check it.
  • • Practice variations: Once you've solved an exercise, try a similar question from a different chapter.
