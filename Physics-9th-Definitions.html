Chapter 1 – Physical Quantities and Measurements

Fundamental & derived quantities, units, precision

New 2025−

Physical Quantities

Quantities that can be measured directly or indirectly using tools and instruments.

SI Units

A system of measurement consisting of seven base units: meter (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), ampere (current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount), candela (luminous intensity).

Measurement Tools:
• Metre rule (least count: 1 mm)
• Vernier Calliper (least count: 0.1 mm)
• Screw gauge (least count: 0.01 mm)
• Stopwatch (least count: 0.1 s)

Chapter 2 – Kinematics

Rest & motion, scalars/vectors, equations of motion

New 2025−

Motion Types

Translatory, rotatory, vibratory, linear, random, and circular motion.

Scalar vs Vector

Scalar quantities have magnitude only; vector quantities have both magnitude and direction.

Kinematic Equations:
\( v = u + at \)
\( s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \)
\( 2as = v^2 - u^2 \)
Speed = \( \frac{Distance}{Time} \), Velocity = \( \frac{Displacement}{Time} \)

Chapter 3 – Dynamics

Newton's laws, momentum, friction, circular motion

New 2025−

Newton's Laws of Motion

1st: Law of inertia, 2nd: F=ma, 3rd: Action-reaction pairs.

Force Types

Contact forces (friction, normal, tension) and non-contact forces (gravity, electromagnetic).

Key Formulas:
• Force: \( F = ma \)
• Momentum: \( p = mv \)
• Impulse: \( F \times \Delta t \)
• Weight: \( w = mg \)

Chapter 4 – Turning Effect of Forces

Torque, equilibrium, center of mass

New 2025−

Torque/Moment of Force

The turning effect of a force about an axis of rotation. \( \tau = F \times l \)

Equilibrium

A body is in equilibrium when net force and net torque are both zero.

Key Concepts:
• Principle of moments
• Center of gravity and center of mass
• Stable, unstable, and neutral equilibrium
• Couple: Two equal and opposite parallel forces

Chapter 5 – Work and Energy

Work, energy forms, conservation, power

New 2025−

Work

Product of force and displacement in the direction of force. \( W = F \times S \times \cos\theta \)

Energy Forms

Kinetic, potential, chemical, thermal, nuclear, and renewable energy sources.

Energy Formulas:
• Kinetic Energy: \( E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \)
• Potential Energy: \( E_p = mgh \)
• Power: \( P = \frac{W}{t} \)
• Efficiency: \( \frac{\text{Useful output}}{\text{Total input}} \times 100\% \)

Chapter 6 – Properties of Matter

Elasticity, pressure, Hooke's law, Archimedes

New 2025−

Elasticity

Property of solids to return to original shape after deforming force is removed.

Pressure

Force exerted normally per unit area. \( P = \frac{F}{A} \)

Key Formulas:
• Density: \( \rho = \frac{m}{V} \)
• Pressure: \( P = \frac{F}{A} \)
• Hooke's Law: \( F = kx \)
• Liquid Pressure: \( P = \rho gh \)
View Full Chapter

Chapter 7 – Thermal Properties of Matter

Temperature, heat, expansion, specific heat

New 2025−

Temperature vs Heat

Temperature measures hotness/coldness; heat is energy transfer due to temperature difference.

Thermometers

Liquid-in-glass thermometers and thermocouples for temperature measurement.

Temperature Scales:
• Celsius: 0°C (ice point) to 100°C (steam point)
• Fahrenheit: 32°F to 212°F
• Kelvin: 273 K to 373 K (absolute scale)
• Absolute Zero: -273°C or 0 K

📖

Chapter 8 – Magnetism

Magnetic materials, field, electromagnetic induction

New 2025−

Magnetic Properties

Like poles repel, unlike poles attract. Magnetic field lines from N to S pole.

Electromagnetism

Electric current produces magnetic field. Right-hand grip rule determines direction.

Magnetic Materials:
• Ferromagnetic (iron, nickel, cobalt)
• Paramagnetic (aluminum, lithium)
• Diamagnetic (copper, bismuth)
• Temporary vs permanent magnets

Chapter 9 – Nature of Science

Scientific method, limitations, role of physics

New 2025−

Scientific Method

Systematic approach: Observation → Hypothesis → Experiment → Theory → Law.

Physics Branches

Mechanics, thermodynamics, optics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, relativity.

Interdisciplinary Fields:
• Biophysics
• Medical Physics
• Astrophysics
• Geophysics
• Climate Physics
• Computational Physics
