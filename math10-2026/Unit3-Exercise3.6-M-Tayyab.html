1 Solve by matrix inversion method, if possible

(i) \(2x + 5y = 19\) ; \(4x - 3y = -1\)

(ii) \(3x + 2y = 7\) ; \(5x - y = 16\)

(iii) \(x - 2y = 9\) ; \(2x + 7y = -4\)

(iv) \(3x + 2y = 2\) ; \(x - 2y = -2\)

(i) \(2x+5y=19\) ; \(4x-3y=-1\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 5 \\ 4 & -3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 19 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ AX = B, \qquad X = A^{-1}B \quad \dots (i) \]

Now

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 5 \\ 4 & -3 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (2)(-3) - (5)(4) = -6 - 20 = -26 \]

\(|A| \neq 0\), A is non singular matrix, so \(A^{-1}\) exist

Therefore

\[ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \operatorname{Adj} A \] \[ \operatorname{Adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} -3 & -5 \\ -4 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{-26} \begin{bmatrix} -3 & -5 \\ -4 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \]

From equation \((i)\)

\[ \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-26} \begin{bmatrix} -3 & -5 \\ -4 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 19 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ = \frac{1}{-26} \begin{bmatrix} (-3)(19)+(-5)(-1) \\ (-4)(19)+(2)(-1) \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-26} \begin{bmatrix} -57+5 \\ -76-2 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-26} \begin{bmatrix} -52 \\ -78 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{-52}{-26} \\ \frac{-78}{-26} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} \]

Thus \(x=2\) and \(y=3\)

\(S.S = \{(2,3)\}\)

(ii) \(3x+2y=7\) ; \(5x-y=16\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 5 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 16 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let \(AX=B\), \(X=A^{-1}B \dots (i)\)

Now

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 5 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (3)(-1) - (2)(5) = -3 - 10 = -13 \]

\(|A| \neq 0\), A is non singular, so \(A^{-1}\) exist

\[ \operatorname{Adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -2 \\ -5 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \]

Therefore

\[ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{-13} \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -2 \\ -5 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \]

From equation (i)

\[ \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-13} \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -2 \\ -5 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 16 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-13} \begin{bmatrix} (-1)(7)+(-2)(16) \\ (-5)(7)+(3)(16) \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-13} \begin{bmatrix} -7-32 \\ -35+48 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-13} \begin{bmatrix} -39 \\ 13 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Thus \(x=3\) and \(y=-1\)

\(S.S = \{(3,-1)\}\)

(iii) \(x-2y=9\) ; \(2x+7y=-4\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 2 & 7 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 9 \\ -4 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let \(AX=B\), \(X=A^{-1}B \dots (i)\)

Now

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 2 & 7 \end{bmatrix}, \quad |A| = (1)(7) - (-2)(2) = 7 - (-4) = 11 \]

\(|A| \neq 0\), so \(A^{-1}\) exists

\[ \operatorname{Adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{11} \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

From (i)

\[ \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{11} \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 9 \\ -4 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{11} \begin{bmatrix} 63 + (-8) \\ -18 + (-4) \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{11} \begin{bmatrix} 55 \\ -22 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} \]

Thus \(x=5\) and \(y=-2\)

\(S.S = \{(5,-2)\}\)

(iv) \(3x+2y=2\) ; \(x-2y=-2\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 1 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let \(AX=B\), \(X=A^{-1}B \dots (i)\)

Now

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 1 & -2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad |A| = (3)(-2) - (2)(1) = -6 - 2 = -8 \]

\(|A| \neq 0\), so \(A^{-1}\) exists

\[ \operatorname{Adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & -2 \\ -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{-8} \begin{bmatrix} -2 & -2 \\ -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \]

From (i)

\[ \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-8} \begin{bmatrix} -2 & -2 \\ -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-8} \begin{bmatrix} (-2)(2)+(-2)(-2) \\ (-1)(2)+(3)(-2) \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-8} \begin{bmatrix} -4+4 \\ -2-6 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{-8} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Thus \(x=0\) and \(y=1\)

\(S.S = \{(0,1)\}\)

2 Use Cramer's rule to solve the following pair of linear equations, if possible

(i) \(x+4y=4\) ; \(2x-y=5\)

(ii) \(x+2y=7\) ; \(3x-2y=1\)

(iii) \(2x-5y=-6\) ; \(4x-5y=-2\)

(iv) \(3x+2y=-1\) ; \(5x+6y=5\)

(i) \(x+4y=4\) ; \(2x-y=5\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 4 \\ 5 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (1)(-1) - (4)(2) = -1 - 8 = -9 \] \[ |A_x| = (4)(-1) - (4)(5) = -4 - 20 = -24 \]

Now

\[ x = \frac{|A_x|}{|A|} = \frac{-24}{-9} = \frac{8}{3} \]

\(A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}\)   (as per PDF, corrected)

\[ |A_y| = (1)(5) - (4)(2) = 5 - 8 = -3 \] \[ y = \frac{|A_y|}{|A|} = \frac{-3}{-9} = \frac{1}{3} \]

Hence \(x = \frac{8}{3}\), \(y = \frac{1}{3}\)

(ii) \(x+2y=7\) ; \(3x-2y=1\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & -2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 2 \\ 1 & -2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 7 \\ 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (1)(-2) - (2)(3) = -2 - 6 = -8 \] \[ |A_x| = (7)(-2) - (2)(1) = -14 - 2 = -16 \] \[ x = \frac{-16}{-8} = 2 \] \[ |A_y| = (1)(1) - (7)(3) = 1 - 21 = -20 \] \[ y = \frac{-20}{-8} = \frac{5}{2} \]

Hence \(S.S = \{(2, \frac{5}{2})\}\)

(iii) \(2x-5y=-6\) ; \(4x-5y=-2\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -5 \\ 4 & -5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -6 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -5 \\ 4 & -5 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} -6 & -5 \\ -2 & -5 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -6 \\ 4 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (2)(-5) - (-5)(4) = -10 + 20 = 10 \] \[ |A_x| = (-6)(-5) - (-5)(-2) = 30 - 10 = 20 \] \[ x = \frac{20}{10} = 2 \] \[ |A_y| = (2)(-2) - (-6)(4) = -4 + 24 = 20 \] \[ y = \frac{20}{10} = 2 \]

Hence \(S.S = \{(2,2)\}\)

(iv) \(3x+2y=-1\) ; \(5x+6y=5\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ 5 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (3)(6) - (2)(5) = 18 - 10 = 8 \] \[ |A_x| = (-1)(6) - (2)(5) = -6 - 10 = -16 \] \[ x = \frac{-16}{8} = -2 \] \[ |A_y| = (3)(5) - (-1)(5) = 15 + 5 = 20 \] \[ y = \frac{20}{8} = \frac{5}{2} \]

Hence \(S.S = \{(-2, \frac{5}{2})\}\)

3 Electrical circuit – current in branches

An electrical engineer wants to determine the current in two branches \(A\) and \(B\) of a simple electrical circuit. The system of the equations is:

\[ x+y=7 \] \[ 2x-y=2 \]

where \(x\) is the current in branch \(A\) and \(y\) is the current in branch \(B\). Find \(x\) and \(y\) by using matrices.

\(x+y=7\)

\(2x-y=2\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 7 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (1)(-1) - (1)(2) = -1 - 2 = -3 \] \[ |A_x| = (7)(-1) - (1)(2) = -7 - 2 = -9 \] \[ x = \frac{|A_x|}{|A|} = \frac{-9}{-3} = 3 \] \[ |A_y| = (1)(2) - (7)(2) = 2 - 14 = -12 \] \[ y = \frac{-12}{-3} = 4 \]

Hence

Current in branch \(A = x = 3\)

Current in branch \(B = y = 4\)

4 Forces in equilibrium

Three forces act on a particle and must be in equilibrium i.e. \(F_1 + F_2 + F_3 = 0\), where \(F_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 8 \\ x \end{bmatrix}\), \(F_2 = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ -7 \end{bmatrix}\), \(F_3 = \begin{bmatrix} y \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}\). Find the value of \(x\) and \(y\).

Since

\[ F_1 + F_2 + F_3 = 0 \] \[ \begin{bmatrix} 8 \\ x \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ -7 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} y \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \begin{bmatrix} 6+y \\ x-8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \]

By comparing

\(6+y = 0 \quad \Rightarrow y = -6\)

\(x-8 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow x = 8\)

Hence \(x=8\), \(y=-6\)

5 Support beams load

Two support beams, \(A\) and \(B\) are holding up a combined load of \(100\,kN\). Twice the load on beam A and three times the load on beam B equals \(240\,kN\). Find the load of beam A and beam B by using matrices.

Let

The load on beam \(A = x\,kN\)

The load on beam \(B = y\,kN\)

According to first condition

\(x+y=100\)

According to second condition

\(2x+3y=240\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 100 \\ 240 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} 100 & 1 \\ 240 & 3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 100 \\ 2 & 240 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (1)(3) - (1)(2) = 3 - 2 = 1 \] \[ |A_x| = (100)(3) - (1)(240) = 300 - 240 = 60 \] \[ x = \frac{60}{1} = 60 \] \[ |A_y| = (1)(240) - (100)(2) = 240 - 200 = 40 \] \[ y = \frac{40}{1} = 40 \]

Hence

Load on beam \(A = x = 60\,kN\)

Load on beam \(B = y = 40\,kN\)

6 2D game world – point of intersection

In a 2D game world, two characters are moving along straight paths. One character moves along a line where the total of twice their horizontal position and vertical position is 5, while the other moves along a line where their horizontal position is one more than their vertical position. Find their point of intersection by using matrices.

Let

The horizontal position \(= x\)

The vertical position \(= y\)

According to first condition

\(2x+y=5\)

According to second condition

\(x = y+1 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x-y = 1\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 5 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (2)(-1) - (1)(1) = -2 - 1 = -3 \] \[ |A_x| = (5)(-1) - (1)(1) = -5 - 1 = -6 \] \[ x = \frac{-6}{-3} = 2 \] \[ |A_y| = (2)(1) - (5)(1) = 2 - 5 = -3 \] \[ y = \frac{-3}{-3} = 1 \]

Hence point of intersection \((x,y) = (2,1)\)

7 Present ages (father & son)

Two years ago a man was 5 times as old as his son was. After 6 years he will be 3 times as old as his son. Find their present ages by using matrices.

Let

The present age of the man \(= x\) years

The present age of the son \(= y\) years

According to first condition (two years ago):

\(x-2 = 5(y-2)\)

\(x-2 = 5y - 10 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x - 5y = -8\)

According to second condition (after 6 years):

\(x+6 = 3(y+6)\)

\(x+6 = 3y + 18 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x - 3y = 12\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -5 \\ 1 & -3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -8 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -5 \\ 1 & -3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} -8 & -5 \\ 12 & -3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -8 \\ 1 & 12 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (1)(-3) - (-5)(1) = -3 + 5 = 2 \] \[ |A_x| = (-8)(-3) - (-5)(12) = 24 - (-60) = 84 \] \[ x = \frac{84}{2} = 42 \] \[ |A_y| = (1)(12) - (-8)(1) = 12 + 8 = 20 \] \[ y = \frac{20}{2} = 10 \]

Hence present age of man \(= 42\) years, son \(= 10\) years.

8 Two cyclists – speeds

Two cyclists are 44 km apart and start out at the same time. If they go towards one another they meet in 2 hours, but if they go in the same direction the faster overtakes the slower in \(7\frac{1}{2}\) hours. Find their speeds by using matrices.

Let

The speed of the faster cyclist \(= x\,kmh^{-1}\)

The speed of the slower cyclist \(= y\,kmh^{-1}\)

According to first condition (moving towards one another):

Distance = Speed × Time

\(44 = (x+y) \times 2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x+y = 22\)

According to second condition (same direction, faster overtakes):

\(44 = (x-y) \times \frac{15}{2}\)

\(44 \times 2 = (x-y) \times 15 \quad \Rightarrow \quad 88 = 15x - 15y\)

\(15x - 15y = 88\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 15 & -15 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 22 \\ 88 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 15 & -15 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} 22 & 1 \\ 88 & -15 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 22 \\ 15 & 88 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (1)(-15) - (1)(15) = -15 - 15 = -30 \] \[ |A_x| = (22)(-15) - (1)(88) = -330 - 88 = -418 \] \[ x = \frac{-418}{-30} = \frac{209}{15} \approx 13.93 \] \[ |A_y| = (1)(88) - (22)(15) = 88 - 330 = -242 \] \[ y = \frac{-242}{-30} = \frac{121}{15} \approx 8.07 \]

Hence speeds: faster \(= \frac{209}{15}\,kmh^{-1}\), slower \(= \frac{121}{15}\,kmh^{-1}\)

(or \(13\frac{14}{15}\) and \(8\frac{1}{15}\) respectively)

9 Fraction problem

The numerator of a fraction is 7 less than the denominator. If the numerator is increased by 3, the new fraction can be cancelled down to \(\frac{3}{4}\). Find the original fraction by using matrices.

Let

The numerator of the fraction \(= x\)

The denominator of the fraction \(= y\)

The original fraction \(= \frac{x}{y}\)

According to first condition: numerator is 7 less than denominator.

\(x = y-7 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x - y = -7\)

According to second condition: if numerator increased by 3,

\(\frac{x+3}{y} = \frac{3}{4}\)

\(4(x+3) = 3y \quad \Rightarrow \quad 4x + 12 = 3y \quad \Rightarrow \quad 4x - 3y = -12\)

In matrix form

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 4 & -3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -7 \\ -12 \end{bmatrix} \]

Let

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 4 & -3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_x = \begin{bmatrix} -7 & -1 \\ -12 & -3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_y = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -7 \\ 4 & -12 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ |A| = (1)(-3) - (-1)(4) = -3 + 4 = 1 \] \[ |A_x| = (-7)(-3) - (-1)(-12) = 21 - 12 = 9 \] \[ x = \frac{9}{1} = 9 \] \[ |A_y| = (1)(-12) - (-7)(4) = -12 + 28 = 16 \] \[ y = \frac{16}{1} = 16 \]

Hence original fraction \(= \frac{9}{16}\)

📐 Key Concepts – Matrix Inversion & Cramer's Rule

Matrix Inversion: \(X = A^{-1}B\), where \(A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|}\text{Adj}(A)\)
Cramer's Rule: \(x = \frac{|A_x|}{|A|}\), \(y = \frac{|A_y|}{|A|}\) for \(2\times2\) systems.
Applications: Electrical circuits, forces, beams, game worlds, ages, cyclists, fractions.
