What this attendance calculator does

This tool has two jobs. First, it computes your current attendance percentage — the simple attended ÷ total × 100 that every school uses. Second, it answers the two questions every student actually asks when their attendance is borderline: "How many classes do I need to attend to reach 75%?" and "How many classes can I still skip without falling below 75%?" Both are solved with closed-form algebra, not guesswork. The result shows a circular progress ring for your percentage, a four-tile summary of the key numbers, and step-by-step arithmetic so you can see the reasoning behind every figure.

Formulas

Current attendance percentage
Attendance % = attended total × 100
Classes needed to reach target (attendance below target)
x ≥ target × total − attended 1 − target
Classes you can still skip (attendance above target)
skips ≤ attended target − total

Variable definitions

  • attended — number of classes you have attended so far.
  • total — total number of classes held so far.
  • target — the attendance percentage you want to reach or maintain, as a decimal (e.g. 75% → 0.75).
  • x — the number of additional classes you must attend to reach the target.
  • skips — the number of classes you can miss while still staying above the target.

The two planning formulas come from solving the same inequality, (attended) / (total + future) ≥ target, in opposite directions. The first solves for the future attended classes (assumes every future class is attended). The second solves for the future skipped classes (assumes no future class is attended).

Worked examples

Example 1: Comfortable attendance

Attended: 78 · Total: 96
  1. Attendance % = 78 / 96 × 100 = 81.25%.
  2. Well above the typical 75% threshold.
  3. Skips allowed to stay at 75%: 78 / 0.75 − 96 = 104 − 96 = 8 classes.

Example 2: Just above the threshold

Attended: 69 · Total: 90
  1. Attendance % = 69 / 90 × 100 = 76.67%.
  2. Above 75% but only just.
  3. Skips allowed to stay at 75%: 69 / 0.75 − 90 = 92 − 90 = 2 classes.
  4. You can miss only two more classes before dropping below 75%.

Example 3: Below the threshold — how many to attend

Attended: 60 · Total: 90 · Target: 75%
  1. Current attendance: 60 / 90 = 66.67%.
  2. Classes needed to reach 75%: (0.75 × 90 − 60) / (1 − 0.75) = (67.5 − 60) / 0.25 = 30 classes.
  3. You need to attend 30 consecutive classes without missing any, and then your attendance will be (60 + 30) / (90 + 30) = 90 / 120 = 75%.
  4. If only 20 classes remain, the target is unreachable this term — the best you can finish with is (60 + 20) / (90 + 20) = 80 / 110 = 72.73%.

Example 4: Attendance exactly at target

Attended: 60 · Total: 80 · Target: 75%
  1. Attendance % = 60 / 80 = 75.00%.
  2. Right on the threshold — no margin.
  3. Skips allowed at 75%: 60 / 0.75 − 80 = 80 − 80 = 0 classes.
  4. Classes needed: 0 — you are already at the target.
  5. You cannot miss any more classes without dropping below 75%.

Example 5: Planning ahead with remaining classes

Attended: 55 · Total: 80 · Target: 75% · Remaining classes: 40
  1. Current attendance: 55 / 80 = 68.75%.
  2. Classes needed: (0.75 × 80 − 55) / (1 − 0.75) = (60 − 55) / 0.25 = 20 classes.
  3. Only 40 classes remain. Since 20 ≤ 40, the target is reachable — you must attend at least 20 of the next 40 without missing.
  4. If you attend all 40, you finish at (55 + 40) / (80 + 40) = 95 / 120 = 79.17%. Still above target.

Example 6: Target unreachable in the remaining term

Attended: 40 · Total: 80 · Target: 75% · Remaining classes: 20
  1. Current attendance: 40 / 80 = 50.00%.
  2. Classes needed: (0.75 × 80 − 40) / 0.25 = (60 − 40) / 0.25 = 80 classes.
  3. Only 20 classes remain. Since 80 > 20, the target is impossible to reach this term.
  4. Best possible finish: (40 + 20) / (80 + 20) = 60 / 100 = 60.00%.
  5. You would need to negotiate with your school about the attendance requirement, or accept the consequences.

Attendance requirements in Pakistan

Attendance rules vary widely between institutions. The most common thresholds:

  • BISE Punjab boards — 75% attendance is the standard minimum to be allowed to sit the annual examination for class 9 and 10. Boards verify attendance via school-submitted records.
  • Government schools — often follow the board minimum (75%), though enforcement varies by school.
  • Private schools — frequently set higher thresholds, such as 80% or 85%. Some premium schools require 90%.
  • Colleges and universities — most Pakistani universities require 75% for exam eligibility, and many professional programmes (MBBS, engineering) require 80%+.
  • Scholarship and honours programmes — some require 90% or higher as a condition for maintaining the award.

When you are unsure, ask your class teacher or check the school's official attendance policy document. Never rely on the general figure alone — the specific rule at your school is what gets applied.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Counting free periods or cancelled classes in the total. Only classes that were actually held count. If a class was cancelled, it is not in the denominator.
  • Forgetting that late arrivals often count as absences. Many schools mark a late student as absent for that period. Check your school's rule.
  • Assuming medical leave counts as attended. Usually it does not, unless the school has an explicit exemption policy. Enter the numbers as your official record shows them.
  • Ignoring the remaining-classes figure. A required-classes number means nothing without knowing whether enough classes remain this term to hit it.
  • Using percentage of working days instead of percentage of class periods. Attendance is usually computed per period, not per day. If your school tracks periods, use per-period numbers.
  • Trusting a rounded percentage at the boundary. 74.5% rounds to 75%, but the official system may not round — you may be genuinely below the cutoff. Keep a small margin above the target.
  • Waiting until the last two weeks to fix attendance. The required-classes formula shows that catching up from below is expensive: dropping to 60% from 75% requires attending roughly three times as many future classes as the gap suggests.

FAQ

How is attendance percentage calculated?▾

Divide the number of classes you attended by the total number of classes conducted, then multiply by 100. For example, if you attended 78 out of 96 classes, your attendance is (78 / 96) × 100 = 81.25%.

How many classes can I miss without falling below a target?▾

The maximum number of additional classes you can miss is found by solving (attended) / (total + skips) ≥ target. Rearranged: skips ≤ attended / target − total. This calculator computes that number for you, given your attended, total and target percentages.

How many classes must I attend to reach a target percentage?▾

If your current attendance is below the target, you need to attend enough consecutive future classes so that (attended + x) / (total + x) ≥ target. Solving for x gives x ≥ (target × total − attended) / (1 − target). The result is rounded up to the next whole class.

What attendance percentage is required in Pakistani schools?▾

Most BISE Punjab boards require 75% attendance to sit the annual exam. Some private schools set higher thresholds (80% or 85%) and a few set lower ones. Check your school's official attendance policy or your board's exam regulations.

Does this calculator count medical leave or approved absences?▾

No. Only the numbers you enter. If your school exempts medical leave or approved absences, subtract those from the total classes and attended classes before entering, so the calculator reflects your official attendance record.

What if the target is impossible to reach?▾

If the required classes to reach the target exceeds the number of classes remaining in the term, the target is mathematically out of reach. The calculator flags this and shows the highest attendance you can still achieve with the remaining classes.
