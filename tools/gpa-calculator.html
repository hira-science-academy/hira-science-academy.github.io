What this GPA calculator does

This calculator works out your grade point average the way most US colleges and high schools do. Choose a letter grade (A+ through F) for each course, enter its credits, and the tool converts each grade to points on the standard 4.0 scale, weights it by credits, and averages the result. For high school, switch to weighted GPA to add Honors, AP, IB or dual-enrollment points. If you enter your current GPA and credits, it also shows your new cumulative GPA.

It is an estimate. Schools differ on how they treat plus/minus grades, A+, repeated courses and weighting, so use your official transcript for anything that is submitted or graded.

How to use it

  1. Choose Unweighted (college, or high school without Honors/AP points) or Weighted (high school with Honors/AP/IB).
  2. For each course, pick the letter grade and enter the credits. College courses are usually 3 or 4 credits. High school courses are often 1 credit for a year or 0.5 for a semester.
  3. In weighted mode, set each course's level to Regular, Honors or AP/IB/Dual.
  4. Optional: open "Add my current cumulative GPA" and enter your GPA and credits so far.
  5. Press Calculate GPA.

GPA formula

GPA (credit-weighted average)
GPA = Σ (grade points × credits) Σ (credits)

Multiply each course's grade points by its credits, add them up, and divide by total credits.

Cumulative GPA
Cumulative = (old GPA × old credits) + (term GPA × term credits) old credits + term credits

Weighted vs unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA treats every course the same, so the maximum is 4.0 (or 4.3 if your school counts A+). A weighted GPA gives extra points for harder classes. The most common system adds 0.5 for Honors and 1.0 for AP, IB or dual-enrollment, so an A in an AP class counts as 5.0 and the weighted maximum is 5.0. The bonus is normally added only when you pass, so this calculator does not add it to an F.

Weighting rules differ between schools and districts. Some use 0.5 and 1.0, some use a flat 5.0 scale for AP, and some do not weight at all. You can edit the two bonus values above to match your school. Many colleges also recalculate your GPA with their own method, often unweighted and using core academic courses only.

Worked examples

Example 1: One college semester (unweighted)

Calculus I (4 credits, A-)
English Composition (3 credits, B+)
General Chemistry (4 credits, B)
US History (3 credits, A)
  1. Grade points × credits: 3.7 × 4 = 14.8; 3.3 × 3 = 9.9; 3.0 × 4 = 12.0; 4.0 × 3 = 12.0.
  2. Sum of quality points: 14.8 + 9.9 + 12.0 + 12.0 = 48.7.
  3. Total credits: 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14.
  4. GPA = 48.7 / 14 = 3.48 (3.4786 before rounding).

Example 2: High school, weighted and unweighted

AP Calculus (1 credit, A, AP)
Honors English (1 credit, A-, Honors)
Spanish II (1 credit, B+, Regular)
AP US History (1 credit, B, AP)
PE (0.5 credit, A, Regular)
  1. Weighted grade points: 4.0 + 1.0 = 5.0; 3.7 + 0.5 = 4.2; 3.3; 3.0 + 1.0 = 4.0; 4.0.
  2. Weighted quality points: 5.0 + 4.2 + 3.3 + 4.0 + (4.0 × 0.5) = 18.5.
  3. Total credits: 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0.5 = 4.5.
  4. Weighted GPA = 18.5 / 4.5 = 4.11.
  5. Unweighted: 4.0 + 3.7 + 3.3 + 3.0 + 2.0 = 16.0, and 16.0 / 4.5 = 3.56.

Example 3: New cumulative GPA

  1. Before this term: GPA 3.20 over 45 credits, so quality points = 3.20 × 45 = 144.
  2. This term: GPA 3.80 over 15 credits, so quality points = 3.80 × 15 = 57.
  3. Cumulative GPA = (144 + 57) / (45 + 15) = 201 / 60 = 3.35.

Example 4: What GPA do I need this semester?

  1. Current: GPA 3.20 over 45 credits (144 quality points). Goal: 3.40 after 15 more credits.
  2. Quality points needed in total: 3.40 × 60 = 204.
  3. Needed this term: 204 − 144 = 60 quality points over 15 credits.
  4. Required term GPA = 60 / 15 = 4.0, which means straight As.
  5. If the answer were above your scale maximum, the goal could not be reached in one term. Plan it over more terms.

What is a good GPA?

A 3.0 is a B average, and a 2.0 is a C average, which is the usual minimum to stay in good academic standing and graduate. Many scholarships ask for a 3.0 to 3.5. Dean's list cutoffs are often around 3.5, and Latin honors such as cum laude, magna cum laude and summa cum laude commonly start somewhere between 3.5 and 3.9, but each school sets its own numbers. For competitive college admissions, applicants often have unweighted GPAs of 3.5 or higher, with the most selective schools seeing much higher averages. A "good" GPA also depends on the course difficulty and your goal, so check the requirements of the program or scholarship you want.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Averaging letters instead of weighting by credits. A 4-credit class counts more than a 1-credit class.
  • Mixing weighted and unweighted values. Use one type for your current GPA and for this term, or the cumulative result will be wrong.
  • Assuming A+ is 4.3. At most schools an A+ is 4.0. Use the A+ setting to match your school.
  • Counting pass/fail courses. These usually earn credit but no grade points. Leave them out unless your school says otherwise.
  • Forgetting repeated courses. Some schools replace the old grade and others average both. Enter the courses the way your school counts them.
  • Rounding too early. Keep full precision during the calculation and round only the final GPA.

FAQ

How is GPA calculated?▾

GPA is a credit-weighted average of your grade points: GPA = Σ(grade points × credits) / Σ(credits). Each letter grade is converted to points on the 4.0 scale (A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0), multiplied by the course credits, added up, and divided by total credits.

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?▾

An unweighted GPA treats every class equally, so the maximum is 4.0. A weighted GPA adds bonus points for harder classes, commonly 0.5 for Honors and 1.0 for AP, IB or dual enrollment, so the maximum is usually 5.0. Weighting rules vary by school.

How much do AP and Honors classes add to GPA?▾

At many US high schools an Honors class adds 0.5 and an AP, IB or dual-enrollment class adds 1.0 to the grade points, so an A in AP counts as 5.0. Some schools use different amounts or do not weight at all, so you can edit the bonus values in the calculator.

What is a good GPA?▾

A 3.0 is a B average and a 2.0 is a C average. Many scholarships ask for 3.0 to 3.5, and dean's list and Latin honors cutoffs are often between 3.5 and 3.9, but every school sets its own. A good GPA depends on your goal and course difficulty.

How do I calculate my cumulative GPA?▾

Multiply your current GPA by your credits so far, add this term's quality points, and divide by total credits: (old GPA × old credits + term quality points) / (old credits + term credits). Open the optional cumulative section in the calculator to do this automatically.

Does an A+ count as 4.3?▾

At most US schools an A+ is worth 4.0, the same as an A. A few schools award 4.3 for an A+. Use the A+ setting in the calculator to match your school.

Do pass/fail and repeated courses count toward GPA?▾

It depends on your school. Pass/fail courses usually earn credit but no grade points, so they are left out of GPA. For repeated courses, some schools replace the old grade and others average both. Enter the courses the way your school counts them.

Can I convert my GPA to a percentage?▾

There is no single official conversion. A common approach is to use your letter grades and their typical percentage ranges, for example A is about 93 to 96 percent and B is about 83 to 86 percent. Use your school's own conversion table if it has one.
