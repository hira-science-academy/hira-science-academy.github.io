What this required exam score calculator does

This tool solves one specific problem: given your current grade, the weight of any single remaining assessment, and your target overall grade, what score do you need on that assessment? It works for a final exam, a midterm you haven't taken yet, a project, a practical, or a viva — anything with a defined weight. The formula is the same one behind the final grade calculator, but this tool is framed for a broader range of uses and places stronger emphasis on impossible-target detection. It computes three numbers you cannot get from a naive calculation: the required score, the ceiling (the highest grade you can still reach with a perfect score), and the floor (the lowest grade you can finish with even if you score 0). Between them, these three numbers answer every version of the question "can I still reach my target?"

Formula

Required score on remaining assessment
Required = Target − Current × (1 − w) w
Ceiling (best possible overall grade)
Ceiling = Current × (1 − w) + 100 × w
Floor (worst possible overall grade)
Floor = Current × (1 − w)

Variable definitions

  • Current — your grade before the remaining assessment, as a percentage.
  • w — the weight of the remaining assessment as a decimal (e.g. 40% → 0.40).
  • Target — the overall grade you want, as a percentage.
  • Required — the score you need on the remaining assessment to hit the target.
  • Ceiling — the highest overall grade reachable (with a perfect 100% on the remaining assessment).
  • Floor — the lowest overall grade possible (with a 0% on the remaining assessment).

Worked examples

Example 1: Reachable target (midterm scenario)

Current: 68%, remaining assessment weight: 40%, target: 75%
  1. Non-remaining contribution: 68 × (1 − 0.40) = 68 × 0.60 = 40.8.
  2. Points needed: 75 − 40.8 = 34.2.
  3. Required score: 34.2 / 0.40 = 85.50%.
  4. Ceiling: 40.8 + 100 × 0.40 = 80.8% (target of 75% is below this, so reachable).
  5. Floor: 40.8 + 0 × 0.40 = 40.8%.
  6. Verdict: reachable. You need 85.5% on the remaining assessment.

Example 2: Impossible target — ceiling below target

Current: 55%, remaining assessment weight: 20%, target: 80%
  1. Non-remaining contribution: 55 × 0.80 = 44.0.
  2. Points needed: 80 − 44 = 36.0.
  3. Required score: 36 / 0.20 = 180% — mathematically above 100%.
  4. Ceiling: 44 + 100 × 0.20 = 64%. This is the highest overall grade possible, and it is 16 points below the target.
  5. Verdict: impossible. Even a perfect 100% leaves you short of 80%.

Example 3: Target already secured — floor above target

Current: 88%, remaining assessment weight: 25%, target: 70%
  1. Non-remaining contribution: 88 × 0.75 = 66.0.
  2. Points needed: 70 − 66 = 4.0.
  3. Required score: 4 / 0.25 = 16%.
  4. Floor: 66 + 0 × 0.25 = 66%. The floor is only 4 points below the target — so the target is reachable, but only if you score above 16%.
  5. Ceiling: 66 + 100 × 0.25 = 91%.
  6. Verdict: reachable. A very low score on the remaining assessment would still leave you near the target.

Example 4: Target fully secured regardless of outcome

Current: 92%, remaining assessment weight: 15%, target: 80%
  1. Non-remaining contribution: 92 × 0.85 = 78.2.
  2. Floor: 78.2 + 0 × 0.15 = 78.2%.
  3. Required score: (80 − 78.2) / 0.15 = 12%.
  4. Since the floor (78.2%) is below the target (80%), the target is not fully secured — you still need at least 12%.
  5. But if the current were 94% instead of 92%, the floor would be 79.9%, still below 80%. Only when the floor reaches or exceeds the target is the target fully secured.

Example 5: Fully secured target (floor ≥ target)

Current: 94%, remaining assessment weight: 15%, target: 75%
  1. Non-remaining contribution: 94 × 0.85 = 79.9.
  2. Floor: 79.9 + 0 = 79.9% — well above the target of 75%.
  3. Required score: (75 − 79.9) / 0.15 = −32.67%, a negative number.
  4. Verdict: target already secured. Even with a 0 on the remaining assessment, you finish at 79.9%, above 75%.

Example 6: Remaining assessment = 100% of grade

Current: 50%, remaining assessment weight: 100%, target: 70%
  1. Non-remaining contribution: 50 × 0 = 0 — the current grade becomes irrelevant.
  2. Required score: (70 − 0) / 1.00 = 70%.
  3. Ceiling: 0 + 100 × 1.00 = 100%. Floor: 0.
  4. Special case worth remembering: when the remaining assessment is the entire course, the required score is exactly the target.

The three-number verdict system

A single required-score number tells only part of the story. This calculator returns three numbers because together they answer every version of "can I still reach my target?":

  • Required score — the score you must achieve on the remaining assessment. Meaningful only when it lies between 0% and 100%.
  • Ceiling — the best possible overall grade, achieved with a perfect 100% on the remaining assessment. If the ceiling is below your target, the target is impossible.
  • Floor — the worst possible overall grade, achieved with 0% on the remaining assessment. If the floor is above your target, the target is already secured.

A target is reachable when Floor ≤ Target ≤ Ceiling. When it is not, the calculator tells you which side you are on and what the closest achievable outcome is.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating a required score above 100% as a valid prediction. It is a signal that the target is impossible with the given weights. The ceiling is the more meaningful number to look at.
  • Treating a negative required score as a bug. It means the target is already secured. The calculator shows this as a positive outcome, with the floor as evidence.
  • Forgetting the remaining assessment may not be the only thing left. If you have more than one assessment remaining, combine their weights and enter them as a single "remaining" block, or use the weighted grade calculator for finer control.
  • Using the wrong weight. If the final is 40% and there is still a midterm worth 20%, do not enter 40% as the "remaining weight" — the remaining weight is 60% (midterm + final combined), and their internal split matters separately.
  • Confusing "current grade" with "target so far". Current grade is what you have earned on everything graded up to now, on a percentage scale, before the remaining assessment.
  • Forgetting that a negative required score still implies a minimum score of 0. You cannot score below 0, so any negative required score is effectively "score anything you can, the target holds".
  • Assuming the target is always achievable because "you can always try harder". Effort is bounded by the 100% ceiling. The ceiling is the hard physical limit.

FAQ

What is a required exam score calculator?▾

It finds the score you need on a single remaining assessment — a final exam, a midterm, a project — to reach a target overall grade. You enter your current grade, the weight of the remaining assessment as a percentage, and your target overall grade, and it returns the score you must achieve.

How is this different from the final grade calculator?▾

The formula is the same. The final grade calculator is framed around the final exam specifically. This calculator works for any single remaining assessment — a midterm, a project, a practical, a viva. It also places stronger emphasis on impossible-target detection, explicitly showing the highest grade you can still reach and the lowest grade you cannot fall below.

What if the calculator says the required score is above 100%?▾

That means no achievable score on the remaining assessment can bring you to your target. The calculator shows the ceiling — the highest overall grade you can still reach with a perfect score — so you can see how far short the target is.

What if the required score is below 0%?▾

That means you have already secured the target. The calculator shows the floor — the lowest overall grade you can finish with even if you score 0 on the remaining assessment — and confirms that this floor is still above your target.

Can the remaining assessment be worth 100% of the grade?▾

Yes. If the remaining assessment is the entire course, the required score equals your target exactly. Your current grade becomes irrelevant, because there is no other assessment to average against.

Why does the calculator reject a weight of 0?▾

A weight of 0 would mean the remaining assessment does not count toward your grade. If it does not count, there is no score you need to hit — the question is undefined. The calculator treats weight 0 as a domain error.
