What this scientific calculator does

This is a full scientific calculator that runs in your browser. It covers what you need for algebra, geometry, trigonometry, pre-calculus, chemistry and physics: sin, cos and tan with a degrees/radians switch, inverse trig (\( \sin^{-1} \), \( \cos^{-1} \), \( \tan^{-1} \)), logarithms (log base 10 and natural ln), powers, roots, factorials, and the constants \( \pi \) and \( e \). It also has a memory register, an Ans key, brackets, and a history of your last 20 calculations. A live preview shows the answer as you type, and pressing \( = \) records the full working below the keypad.

How to use it

  1. Check the angle mode (DEG or RAD) before using trig functions.
  2. Tap the keys or type on your keyboard. Function keys such as sin( open a bracket for you.
  3. Press = (or Enter) to get the result. Any brackets you left open are closed automatically.
  4. After pressing \( = \), type a new number to start over, or press an operator like \( + \) or \( \times \) to keep going from the result.
  5. Use M+, MR and MC to store, recall and clear a value, and click any history entry to reuse it.

Degrees vs radians

Angles can be measured in degrees (a full circle is \( 360^\circ \)) or radians (a full circle is \( 2\pi \)). Most high school geometry and trigonometry work uses degrees, while calculus, AP Calculus and many physics problems use radians. The calculator starts in DEG mode. To convert by hand:

Conversion
\( \text{radians} = \text{degrees} \times \frac{\pi}{180} \)
Trig in DEG mode
\( \sin\theta = \sin\left(\theta \times \frac{\pi}{180}\right) \)
Logarithms and roots
\( \log x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln 10}, \qquad \sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n} \)

Order of operations (PEMDAS)

The calculator follows PEMDAS: Parentheses first, then Exponents, then Multiplication and Division from left to right, then Addition and Subtraction from left to right. Exponents stack from the right, so \( 2^{3^2} = 2^{9} = 512 \). A minus sign in front of a power is applied after the power, so \( -2^2 = -4 \), while \( (-2)^2 = 4 \).

Worked examples

Example 1: \( \sin(30^\circ) \) in degree mode

  1. Make sure the mode shows DEG.
  2. Enter sin(30) and press \( = \).
  3. Result: 0.5. In RAD mode the same keys give about \( -0.98803 \), so the mode matters.

Example 2: The height of a tree

You stand 50 feet from a tree and the angle up to the top is \( 40^\circ \).

  1. \( \text{Height} = \text{distance} \times \tan(\text{angle}) = 50 \times \tan(40^\circ) \).
  2. Enter 50*tan(40) in DEG mode.
  3. Result: about 41.95 feet.

Example 3: Compound interest

$1,000 at 5% annual interest, compounded monthly for 10 years.

  1. \( A = P\left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt} \) with \( P=1000,\ r=0.05,\ n=12,\ t=10 \).
  2. Enter 1000*(1+0.05/12)^120.
  3. Result: about $1,647.01.

Example 4: Logs and powers together

  1. Enter log(1000)+2^3.
  2. \( \log(1000) = 3 \) and \( 2^3 = 8 \).
  3. Result: 3 + 8 = 11.

Example 5: Cube roots, including negatives

  1. \( 27^{1/3} = 3 \).
  2. \( (-8)^{1/3} = -2 \), because \( -2 \times -2 \times -2 = -8 \). Odd roots of negative numbers are real.
  3. \( (-8)^{1/2} \) shows an error, because an even root of a negative number is not real.

Example 6: Negatives and exponents

  1. \( -2^2 = -4 \) (the power is done first).
  2. \( (-2)^2 = 4 \).
  3. \( 10^{-3} = 0.001 \).

Example 7: Inverse trig and factorials

  1. \( \tan^{-1}(1) = 45 \) in DEG mode, the angle whose tangent is \( 1 \).
  2. Ordered arrangements of 3 items from 8: \( \frac{8!}{(8-3)!} = \frac{40320}{120} = 336 \).

Example 8: Keep going with the last answer

  1. Enter 5+3 and press \( = \). Result: 8.
  2. Now press \( \times \) then 2 and press \( = \). The calculator continues from 8.
  3. Result: 16.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Wrong angle mode. If \( \sin(30) \) shows about \( -0.988 \) instead of \( 0.5 \), you are in RAD mode. Check the DEG/RAD indicator first.
  • Confusing log and ln. log is base 10 and ln is base \( e \). Use the change-of-base rule for other bases, such as \( \frac{\log 8}{\log 2} \) for log base 2 of 8.
  • Inverse trig out of range. \( \sin^{-1} \) and \( \cos^{-1} \) only accept values from \( -1 \) to \( 1 \).
  • Forgetting brackets around a fraction exponent. Write \( 8^{1/3} \), not \( 8^1/3 \), which means \( (8^1)/3 \).
  • Typing a negative base without brackets. \( -3^2 = -9 \) but \( (-3)^2 = 9 \).
  • Even roots of negative numbers. These have no real result, so the calculator shows an error.

FAQ

How do I switch between degrees and radians?▾

Use the DEG/RAD toggle above the display. DEG is the default and is what most high school geometry and trigonometry classes use. Switch to RAD for calculus, physics problems that use radians, and any formula with angles written in terms of \( \pi \). To convert by hand, \( \text{radians} = \text{degrees} \times \pi \div 180 \).

How do I find inverse trig values like sin⁻¹?▾

Use the \( \sin^{-1} \), \( \cos^{-1} \) and \( \tan^{-1} \) keys, which appear as asin, acos and atan in the expression. For example, \( \tan^{-1}(1) \) gives 45 in DEG mode and about 0.7854 in RAD mode. asin and acos only accept values from \( -1 \) to \( 1 \).

How do I enter exponents, roots and negative powers?▾

Use ^ for powers: \( 2^{10} = 1024 \) and \( 10^{-3} = 0.001 \). Use the square root key or sqrt( for square roots, and a fractional exponent for other roots, for example \( 27^{1/3} = 3 \). Odd roots of negative numbers work too, so \( (-8)^{1/3} = -2 \). Exponents stack from the right, so \( 2^{3^2} = 2^{9} = 512 \).

What is the difference between log and ln?▾

\( \log \) is the base-10 logarithm and \( \ln \) is the natural logarithm (base \( e \)). This matches most US scientific calculators. For another base, use the change-of-base rule: log base 2 of 8 is \( \frac{\log 8}{\log 2} = 3 \).

How do I enter scientific notation?▾

There is no EE key. Type the number times a power of 10, for example \( 6.02 \times 10^{23} \). Very large or very small results are also shown in scientific notation in the result panel.

Does it follow the order of operations (PEMDAS)?▾

Yes. Brackets come first, then exponents, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. A minus sign in front of a power is applied after the power, so \( -2^2 = -4 \) while \( (-2)^2 = 4 \).

Can I use this calculator on the SAT, ACT or AP exams?▾

It is meant for homework and practice. Standardized tests have their own calculator rules, and online tools are generally not allowed in the test room. Some digital exams provide a built-in calculator, so check the current rules from the test provider.

What do Ans and the memory keys do, and is my work saved?▾

Ans inserts your last result, and after pressing \( = \) you can keep going by pressing an operator such as \( + \) or \( \times \). M+ adds the current value to memory, MR recalls it and MC clears it. The last 20 calculations appear in the history panel. Everything stays in your browser tab and is cleared when you reload the page.
