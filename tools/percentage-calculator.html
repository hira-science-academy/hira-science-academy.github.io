What this percentage calculator does

This calculator answers the percentage questions people ask most often. It finds a percent of a number (what is 15% of 250?), works out what percent one number is of another (42 out of 50 is what percent?), adds or subtracts a percentage (add 8.25% sales tax, take 30% off a price), and calculates percentage increase, decrease and change between two values. A percentage difference mode compares two numbers when neither is the original. Every result comes with the formula and the steps, so you can check your homework or understand the math, not just copy the answer.

Percentage formulas

Percent of a number
\[ \text{Result} = \frac{p}{100} \times n \]
X is what percent of Y
\[ \text{Percentage} = \frac{X}{Y} \times 100 \]
Add or subtract a percent (tax, tip, discount)
\[ \text{New value} = n \times \left(1 \pm \frac{p}{100}\right) \]
Percentage change (increase or decrease)
\[ \text{Change \%} = \frac{Y - X}{X} \times 100 \]
Percentage difference
\[ \text{Difference \%} = \frac{|X - Y|}{\dfrac{X + Y}{2}} \times 100 \]

Variable definitions

  • p is the percentage rate (use 15 for 15%).
  • n is the number you take a percentage of, such as a price or bill.
  • X is the original or first value, and the base for percentage change.
  • Y is the new or second value.

Worked examples

Example 1: Percent off. 30% off an $80 jacket

  1. Savings = 0.30 × 80 = $24.
  2. Sale price = 80 − 24 = $56. (Or in one step: 80 × 0.70 = 56.)

Example 2: Sales tax. 8.25% tax on a $45 purchase

  1. Tax = 0.0825 × 45 = 3.7125, which rounds to $3.71.
  2. Total = 45 + 3.7125 = 48.7125, which rounds to $48.71. (Or: 45 × 1.0825.)

Example 3: Tip. 18% on a $62.50 restaurant bill

  1. Tip = 0.18 × 62.50 = $11.25.
  2. Total = 62.50 + 11.25 = $73.75.

Example 4: Test score. 42 out of 50 is what percent?

  1. (42 ÷ 50) × 100 = 0.84 × 100 = 84%.

Example 5: Percent increase. A raise from $55,000 to $57,750

  1. Change = 57,750 − 55,000 = 2,750.
  2. 2,750 ÷ 55,000 = 0.05, and 0.05 × 100 = 5% increase.

Example 6: Percent decrease. A price drops from 100 to 75

  1. (75 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 = −25%, a 25% decrease.
  2. To get back from 75 to 100 you need (100 − 75) ÷ 75 × 100 = 33.3%. A drop and a rise of the same percent do not cancel out, because they use different bases.

Example 7: Percentage difference between 40 and 60

  1. |40 − 60| = 20, and the average is (40 + 60) ÷ 2 = 50.
  2. (20 ÷ 50) × 100 = 40%. Swapping the two numbers gives the same answer.

Quick mental math for percentages

  • 10% of any number: move the decimal one place left. 10% of $85 is $8.50.
  • 5% is half of 10%, so 5% of $85 is $4.25.
  • 15% is 10% plus 5%, so 15% of $85 is $12.75. This is a fast way to figure a tip.
  • 20% is double 10%, so 20% of $85 is $17.00.
  • 1% is the decimal moved two places left, which you can multiply for other values.

When to use which mode

  • What is X% of Y: a tip, a commission, a portion of a total, or how many points a test section is worth.
  • X is what percent of Y: a test or assignment score, or what share one amount is of a budget.
  • Add X% to Y: sales tax, a tip added to a bill, a markup, or a price after an increase.
  • Subtract X% from Y: a sale price, coupon, or "percent off" discount.
  • Percentage increase, decrease and change: raises, price changes, growth, stock or home price moves.
  • Percentage difference: comparing two measurements when neither is the original, such as two lab results.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using the wrong base. Percentage change divides by the original value, not the new one.
  • Mixing up percent and percentage points. Going from 20% to 25% is 5 percentage points, but a 25% relative increase.
  • Stacking percentages. A 10% increase followed by a 10% decrease does not return to the start, because the second percent applies to a larger number.
  • Applying discounts and tax in the wrong order. In most US stores the discount is taken first and sales tax is added to the lower price. Rules differ by state and store.
  • Dropping the sign. A negative percentage change means the value went down.
  • Percent change from zero. It is undefined, so describe the change in absolute terms.
  • Rounding too early. Keep full precision during the calculation and round only the final dollar amount.

FAQ

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?▾

Divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the number. For example, 15% of 250 is 0.15 × 250 = 37.5. To find 10% of any number, move the decimal point one place to the left.

How do I calculate a discount or percent off?▾

Multiply the original price by the discount rate to get the savings, then subtract it from the price. A 30% discount on $80 saves 0.30 × 80 = $24, so the sale price is $56. You can also multiply the price by 0.70 in one step.

How do I add sales tax to a price?▾

Multiply the price by (1 + tax rate). With 8.25% sales tax, a $45 item costs 45 × 1.0825 = $48.71. Sales tax rates differ by state, county and city, so use the rate for your location.

How do I calculate a tip?▾

Multiply the bill by the tip percentage. An 18% tip on a $62.50 bill is 0.18 × 62.50 = $11.25, so the total is $73.75. A quick method is to take 10% by moving the decimal, then add half of that for 15% or double it for 20%.

How do I calculate percentage increase?▾

Subtract the original value from the new value, divide by the original, then multiply by 100. A raise from $55,000 to $57,750 is (57,750 − 55,000) ÷ 55,000 × 100 = 5%.

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage difference?▾

Percentage change uses the starting value as the base: (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Percentage difference uses the average of the two values as the base: |a − b| ÷ ((a + b) ÷ 2) × 100. Use change when one value is clearly the original, and difference when neither is a reference point.

Can a percentage change be negative, and what if the starting value is zero?▾

Yes. A negative result means a decrease, for example 100 to 75 is −25%. Percentage change from zero is undefined because it needs division by zero, so this calculator shows an error and you should describe the change in absolute terms instead.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?▾

Percentage points describe the simple gap between two percentages, while percent describes a relative change. If an interest rate rises from 4% to 5%, that is 1 percentage point, but a 25% relative increase.
