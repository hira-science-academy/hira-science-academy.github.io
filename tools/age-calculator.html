What is a chronological age calculator?

A chronological age calculator determines a person's exact age in years, months, and days between their date of birth and a testing or assessment date. It is a core tool for psychologists, speech-language pathologists (SLPs), special educators, and researchers who administer norm-referenced assessments such as the WISC-V, KTEA-3, BASC-3, CELF-5, and the Woodcock-Johnson.

The result is typically written in Y;M;D format (for example, 7;10 for 7 years and 10 months) and is used to select the correct age-based norm group from the test manual. The calculator also gives total days, weeks, months, and hours lived for record-keeping and milestone tracking.

The Pearson chronological age calculator has been discontinued

Pearson Assessments no longer offers a live chronological age calculator on its website. The official Pearson page now says, in its own words, that it no longer has a live age calculator and redirects visitors to a generic third-party duration calculator on timeanddate.com — a tool that is not designed for clinical or assessment use.

Why chronological age matters in testing

Standardized assessments are scored against age-specific norms. A miscalculated chronological age — even by one month — can place the examinee in the wrong norm band, which can:

  • Produce an invalid or misleading standard score.
  • Lead to an under- or over-identification of a disability.
  • Compromise the defensibility of the evaluation in an IEP meeting or due-process hearing.

Because of this, many professionals prefer to calculate age in Y;M;D format rather than relying on a rough "years and months" estimate. This calculator follows the strict calendar-arithmetic method used in clinical reports.

How to calculate chronological age manually

Chronological age is counted in calendar units, not by dividing a number of days by 365. Write both dates in year-month-day order and subtract:

Steps
  1. Subtract the birth days from the testing days. If the testing day is smaller, borrow one month (add the number of days in the previous month to the testing day, and subtract 1 from the testing month).
  2. Subtract the birth months from the testing months. If the testing month is smaller, borrow 12 months (add 12 to the testing month, and subtract 1 from the testing year).
  3. Subtract the birth years from the testing years.
  4. Write the result as Years;Months;Days — for example, 7;10;3 means 7 years, 10 months, and 3 days. In clinical reports, the days are often dropped, giving 7;10.

Worked example 1 — testing date later in the month

Born August 15, 2005 · Tested October 9, 2026

  1. Days: 9 − 15 → borrow one month. 9 + 30 (September has 30 days) = 39. 39 − 15 = 24 days.
  2. Months: 9 (October) became 9 − 1 = 8. 8 − 8 = 0 months.
  3. Years: 2026 − 2005 = 21 years.
  4. Result: 21;0;24 (21 years, 0 months, 24 days), which is 7,725 days in total.

Worked example 2 — testing month earlier than birth month

Born June 15, 2015 · Tested February 5, 2024

  1. Days: 5 − 15 → borrow one month. 5 + 31 (January has 31 days) = 36. 36 − 15 = 21 days.
  2. Months: February (2) became 1. 1 − 6 → borrow 12 months. 1 + 12 = 13. 13 − 6 = 7 months.
  3. Years: 2024 became 2023 (due to borrowing). 2023 − 2015 = 8 years.
  4. Result: 8;7;21 — written as 8;7 in a clinical report.

Worked example 3 — February 29 birth date

Born February 29, 2004 · Tested February 28, 2025

  1. February 29, 2004 → January 29, 2025 = 20 years, 11 months.
  2. January 29 → February 28, 2025 = 30 days.
  3. Result: 20;11;30 (7,670 days). In non-leap years, the day a leap-day birthday officially counts (February 28 or March 1) can vary by state or organization, so check the rule that applies to your setting.

Y;M;D vs. decimal age — which should I use?

Most assessment manuals express norms in Y;M;D (years;months;days) — for example, "7 years, 10 months." Use Y;M;D when you are selecting a norm group or writing a clinical report. Some research protocols and data-management systems instead require a decimal age (e.g., 7.83). To convert, divide the months by 12 and add to the years; this calculator also shows the total months so you can convert easily.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a chronological age calculator?▾

A chronological age calculator determines a person's exact age in years, months, and days between their date of birth and a testing or assessment date. It is used by psychologists, speech-language pathologists, and special educators to identify the correct norm group for standardized assessments such as the WISC-V, KTEA-3, BASC-3, and CELF-5.

Why did Pearson discontinue its chronological age calculator?▾

Pearson Assessments no longer offers a live chronological age calculator on its website. The official Pearson page now redirects users to a generic third-party duration calculator on timeanddate.com. Professionals who relied on the Pearson tool now need a reliable, free, and privacy-respecting alternative — which is exactly what this calculator provides.

How is chronological age used in psychological and educational testing?▾

Chronological age determines which age-based norm group is used to score a standardized test. For example, the WISC-V, KTEA-3, BASC-3, and CELF-5 all use age-specific norms. A miscalculated chronological age — even by one month — can place the examinee in the wrong norm band and lead to a misdiagnosis or an invalid score.

How do I calculate chronological age manually?▾

Write the testing date and date of birth in year-month-day format, then subtract the days, months, and years separately. When the testing day is earlier than the birth day, borrow one month (add 30 to the testing day and subtract 1 from the testing month). When the testing month is earlier than the birth month, borrow 12 months (add 12 to the testing month and subtract 1 from the testing year). Write the final result as years;months (for example, 7;10).

What is the Y;M;D format and why is it used?▾

Y;M;D (or Y:M:D) is the standard notation used in psychoeducational reports to express chronological age as years, months, and days, separated by semicolons or colons. For example, 7;10 means 7 years and 10 months. This format allows a clinician to compare the examinee's age directly to the norm tables in the test manual.

How does the calculator handle February 29 birthdays?▾

The calculator counts exact calendar days using UTC dates, so leap years are handled correctly. For people born on February 29, this calculator follows strict calendar arithmetic. Which day counts as the birthday in non-leap years can vary by state, school district, or organization, so check the rule that applies to your setting for official purposes.

Is my date of birth or client data saved or sent anywhere?▾

No. The calculation runs entirely in your web browser. The dates you enter are not stored, transmitted, or tracked on any server. This makes the tool safe for use with sensitive client information.

Why might another age calculator show a different result?▾

Some calculators count the day of birth as day one, use the local time zone, or treat month lengths differently. This tool uses strict calendar arithmetic on dates only, with no time zone, which matches how age is normally counted on clinical forms and assessment protocols.
