What this pressure calculator does

This tool applies the pressure formula P = F / A in all three of its rearrangements. If you know force and area, it computes pressure. If you know pressure and area, it computes force. If you know force and pressure, it computes area. Inputs are unit-aware: force in newtons, kilonewtons or kilogram-force; area in square metres, square centimetres or square millimetres; pressure output in pascals, kilopascals or atmospheres. Beyond the number, the calculator places the result against real-world reference points — atmospheric pressure, car tyre pressure, blood pressure, hydraulic system pressure — so you can tell immediately whether a result is reasonable or a unit error.

Formulas — three rearrangements

Find pressure
P = F A
Find force
F = P × A
Find area
A = F P

Variable definitions

  • P — pressure, in pascals (Pa), kilopascals (kPa), or atmospheres (atm).
  • F — force perpendicular to the surface, in newtons (N).
  • A — area over which the force is applied, in square metres (m²).

Unit conversions you need to know

  • 1 Pa = 1 N/m²
  • 1 kPa = 1000 Pa
  • 1 atm = 101,325 Pa ≈ 101.3 kPa
  • 1 kgf (kilogram-force) = 9.8 N
  • 1 kN = 1000 N
  • 1 m² = 10,000 cm² = 1,000,000 mm²

Pressure is a scalar — it has no direction, only magnitude. The force, however, must act perpendicular to the surface. A force acting at an angle contributes only its perpendicular component to the pressure.

Worked examples

Example 1: Find pressure — a box on the floor

F = 500 N · A = 0.5 m²
  1. P = F / A = 500 / 0.5.
  2. P = 1000 Pa = 1 kPa.
  3. A 50 kg box (weight ≈ 490 N) resting on 0.5 m² exerts about 1 kPa on the floor — very comfortable for any building.

Example 2: Find pressure — a nail tip

F = 50 N · A = 0.01 mm² = 1 × 10⁻⁸ m²
  1. P = 50 / 1e-8.
  2. P = 5 × 10⁹ Pa = 5 GPa = about 49,000 atm.
  3. A sharp nail tip concentrates the force into an area 50,000 times smaller than 0.5 m², producing a pressure 5 million times higher. This is why nails penetrate wood with a modest hammer tap.

Example 3: Find pressure — a car tyre contact patch

F = 4000 N (one tyre bearing a quarter of the car's weight) · A = 0.02 m²
  1. P = 4000 / 0.02.
  2. P = 200,000 Pa = 200 kPa.
  3. 200 kPa is a typical car tyre gauge pressure — the calculation and reality match.

Example 4: Find force — atmospheric pressure on a window

P = 101,325 Pa · A = 1 m²
  1. F = P × A = 101325 × 1.
  2. F = 101,325 N ≈ 10.3 tonnes-force.
  3. A 1 m² window experiences about 10 tonnes of atmospheric pressure. It does not shatter because the pressure inside the room is the same.

Example 5: Find force — hydraulic press

P = 5,000,000 Pa · A = 0.01 m²
  1. F = P × A = 5000000 × 0.01.
  2. F = 50,000 N = 50 kN ≈ 5.1 tonnes-force.
  3. A hydraulic press at 5 MPa on a 0.01 m² piston produces 5 tonnes of force — this is how a small pump pressure can lift a car.

Example 6: Find area — a foundation pad

F = 500,000 N (weight of a small building) · P = 200,000 Pa (allowable soil pressure)
  1. A = F / P = 500000 / 200000.
  2. A = 2.5 m².
  3. A building weighing 500 kN needs a foundation pad of at least 2.5 m² to keep soil pressure at 200 kPa. This is why foundations are wider than the walls they support.

Pressure in perspective

Pressure is one of those quantities where knowing typical values is more useful than knowing the formula alone. These reference points help you sanity-check any result.

  • ~0.1 Pa — sound pressure in a very quiet room.
  • ~100 Pa — sound pressure during normal conversation.
  • ~1000 Pa = 1 kPa — a light breeze; also a box on the floor.
  • ~10 kPa — a person standing on two feet.
  • ~16 kPa — normal systolic blood pressure (120 mmHg).
  • ~101 kPa = 1 atm — atmospheric pressure at sea level.
  • ~200 kPa — car tyre gauge pressure.
  • ~1 MPa — high-pressure household water supply.
  • ~10 MPa — hydraulic systems, deep water (1000 m depth).
  • ~100 GPa — pressure at Earth's core.

Notice how wide the range is — from 0.1 Pa to 100 GPa spans 12 orders of magnitude. The correct unit choice (Pa, kPa, MPa, atm) matters as much as the number itself.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing cm² with m² or forgetting the square. 1 m² = 10,000 cm², not 100 cm². Squaring a length conversion factor gives the area conversion factor. Mixing 5 cm² with 1 Pa gives a result 10,000× wrong.
  • Using mass instead of force. The formula uses force in newtons. A 5 kg mass has a weight of 5 × 9.8 = 49 N. The calculator converts kgf to N automatically if you choose that unit, but you must know the difference.
  • Confusing gauge pressure with absolute pressure. Tyre gauges read gauge pressure (the excess above atmospheric). Absolute pressure = gauge + atmospheric. If a problem says "absolute", add 101 kPa.
  • Assuming pressure depends only on force. Doubling the force doubles the pressure, but halving the area also doubles it. Both matter. A sharp knife works because of the small area, not a large force.
  • Using the wrong angle for non-perpendicular forces. The formula P = F / A uses the component of force perpendicular to the surface. A force acting at 45° contributes only its perpendicular component.
  • Setting area to zero. Division by zero is undefined. The calculator flags this as a domain error — a zero area would mean infinite pressure, which is physically impossible.
  • Forgetting that atmospheric pressure pushes in all directions. It is not just downward. This is why you do not feel it — the pressure inside your body pushes outward with the same magnitude.

FAQ

What is the formula for pressure?▾

Pressure is force per unit area: P = F / A. The SI unit is the pascal (Pa), where 1 Pa = 1 newton per square metre (N/m²). Pressure increases when force increases or when area decreases.

Why does a sharp knife cut better than a blunt one?▾

A sharp knife has a much smaller contact area. For the same force applied by your hand, pressure = force / area, so a smaller area produces a much larger pressure. The higher pressure concentrates the cutting effect on a narrow line, allowing it to slice through material that a blunt blade would only squash.

What is atmospheric pressure?▾

Atmospheric pressure is the pressure exerted by the weight of air above a surface. At sea level, standard atmospheric pressure is 101,325 Pa (about 101.3 kPa or 1 atm). This is equivalent to about 10 newtons pressing on every square centimetre of surface — enough to crush an empty can.

What is the pressure of normal car tyres?▾

Typical car tyre pressures are around 30 to 35 psi (pounds per square inch) or roughly 200 to 240 kPa. Motorcycle tyres are usually a bit lower at 25 to 30 psi, and bicycle tyres range from 40 psi to over 100 psi depending on the type. These values are gauge pressure — the pressure above atmospheric.

What is the difference between gauge pressure and absolute pressure?▾

Gauge pressure measures the difference from atmospheric pressure. Absolute pressure measures from a complete vacuum. Absolute pressure = gauge pressure + atmospheric pressure. A car tyre reading 200 kPa on a gauge has an absolute pressure of about 300 kPa (200 + 101 kPa).

Why can area not be zero in the pressure formula?▾

Area is in the denominator of P = F / A. Dividing by zero is undefined, and physically a zero area would mean the force is concentrated on a mathematical point — mathematically infinite pressure. The calculator treats area = 0 as a domain error and explains why.
