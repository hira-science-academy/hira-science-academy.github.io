What is the greatest common factor (GCF)?

The greatest common factor (GCF) — also called the highest common factor (HCF) or greatest common divisor (GCD) — is the largest whole number that divides evenly into every number in a list. For example, the GCF of 12 and 18 is 6, because 6 is the largest number that divides both 12 and 18 without a remainder. All three names (GCF, HCF, GCD) refer to exactly the same value; different regions and textbooks prefer different terms. You may occasionally see the phrase “highest common multiple” — that is a common misnomer for HCF, since multiples go on forever and have no highest value. The correct concept is the highest common factor. Similarly, “lowest common factor” is not a standard term: the lowest common factor of any two positive numbers is always 1.

How to find the GCF

There are three common methods. Each gives the same answer.

1. Listing factors

List all factors of each number, then pick the largest one that appears in every list. For 12 and 18: factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12; factors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. The common factors are 1, 2, 3, 6, so the GCF is 6.

Real-world uses

  • Simplifying fractions: divide numerator and denominator by their GCF. Use the Fraction Calculator to check your work.
  • Tiling and packaging: cutting a rectangular floor into equal square tiles uses the GCF of the side lengths.
  • Grouping: dividing items into equal groups with no leftovers uses the GCF.
  • Scheduling recurring events: buses, bells or alarms that repeat at different intervals meet again after the LCM of the intervals.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Confusing GCF with LCM. GCF ≤ smallest input; LCM ≥ largest input.
  • Applying the GCF-LCM identity to three or more numbers. It holds only for two numbers.
  • Stopping Euclid too early. Continue until the remainder is 0.
  • Missing a prime factor in LCM. Use the highest power of every prime that appears.
  • Using negative numbers. GCF and LCM are defined for positive integers.
  • Assuming GCF = 1 means the numbers are prime. They are coprime, but neither has to be prime (e.g. 8 and 9).

FAQ

What is the difference between GCF and LCM?▾

The greatest common factor (GCF, also called HCF or GCD) is the largest number that divides evenly into all the numbers. The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that all the numbers divide evenly into. For 12 and 18, GCF is 6 and LCM is 36.

How are GCF and LCM related?▾

For two positive integers a and b, GCF(a, b) times LCM(a, b) equals a times b. For 12 and 18: GCF = 6, LCM = 36, and 6 times 36 = 216 = 12 times 18. This relationship does not extend directly to three or more numbers.

How do I find the GCF using Euclid's algorithm?▾

Repeatedly replace the larger number with the remainder when the larger is divided by the smaller. When the remainder is zero, the last non-zero divisor is the GCF. For 48 and 18: 48 mod 18 = 12, 18 mod 12 = 6, 12 mod 6 = 0, so GCF = 6.

How do I find the LCM using prime factorisation?▾

Write each number as a product of primes, then take the highest power of each prime that appears. For 12 = 2 squared times 3 and 18 = 2 times 3 squared, the LCM is 2 squared times 3 squared = 4 times 9 = 36.

Can LCM and GCF be found for more than two numbers?▾

Yes. For GCF, apply Euclid's algorithm iteratively: GCF(a, b, c) = GCF(GCF(a, b), c). For LCM, use LCM(a, b, c) = LCM(LCM(a, b), c). This calculator accepts up to ten numbers.

What if all the numbers are zero?▾

GCF of any set that includes zero is defined as the GCF of the non-zero numbers. If all numbers are zero, both GCF and LCM are undefined. LCM of a set that includes zero is zero.

Is HCF the same as GCF?▾

Yes. HCF (highest common factor) is the same as GCF (greatest common factor) and GCD (greatest common divisor). Different regions and textbooks use different names for the same value.

What is the GCF of two prime numbers?▾

The GCF of two distinct prime numbers is always 1, because they share no common factor other than 1. For example, GCF(7, 13) = 1.

What is the GCF of 0 and a number?▾

GCF(0, n) = n for any positive integer n, because every number divides 0 and n divides itself. If all numbers are zero, the GCF is undefined.

How do I simplify a fraction using the GCF?▾

Find the GCF of the numerator and denominator, then divide both by it. For example, to simplify 24/36: GCF(24, 36) = 12, so 24 ÷ 12 = 2 and 36 ÷ 12 = 3, giving 2/3.
