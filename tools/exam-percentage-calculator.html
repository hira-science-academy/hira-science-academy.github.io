What this exam percentage calculator does

This tool converts raw exam marks into a percentage — the standard "obtained over total, times 100" conversion used in every Pakistani board, school and university. You enter the marks you achieved and the maximum marks possible, and the calculator returns the percentage, the grade band (first, second, third division, or fail), and the step-by-step arithmetic. For BISE Punjab class 9 and 10 subjects that include a theory paper and a practical, tick the combine checkbox to enter the two components separately. The calculator adds them first, then computes the combined percentage — so you see both the individual components and the total.

Formula

Percentage from marks
Percentage = Obtained marks Total marks × 100
Combined (theory + practical)
Percentage = Theoryobt + Practicalobt Theorytotal + Practicaltotal × 100

Variable definitions

  • Obtained marks — the marks you scored in the exam.
  • Total marks — the maximum marks possible in the exam.
  • Theoryobt, Theorytotal — marks from the theory paper.
  • Practicalobt, Practicaltotal — marks from the practical exam.

BISE Punjab grading bands

The following bands apply to class 9 and 10 results in most BISE Punjab boards. Exact thresholds can vary slightly by year and board — always confirm with your board's notification.

  • 80% and above — A+ grade (excellent).
  • 70% to 79.99% — A grade (very good).
  • 60% to 69.99% — B grade (first division).
  • 50% to 59.99% — C grade (second division).
  • 40% to 49.99% — D grade (third division).
  • 33% to 39.99% — E grade (pass).
  • Below 33% — Fail.

Worked examples

Example 1: A single paper

Obtained: 68 · Total: 85
  1. Fraction: 68 / 85 = 0.800.
  2. Percentage: 0.800 × 100 = 80.00%.
  3. Band: A grade (above 80%).

Example 2: A mid-range result

Obtained: 47 · Total: 75
  1. Fraction: 47 / 75 = 0.6267.
  2. Percentage: 62.67%.
  3. Band: B grade (first division, 60–69.99%).

Example 3: Theory + practical (Physics, BISE style)

Theory: 55 / 70 · Practical: 18 / 30
  1. Theory percentage: 55 / 70 = 78.57%.
  2. Practical percentage: 18 / 30 = 60.00%.
  3. Combined obtained: 55 + 18 = 73.
  4. Combined total: 70 + 30 = 100.
  5. Combined percentage: 73 / 100 = 73.00%.
  6. Band: A grade (above 70%). Note the combined percentage (73%) sits between the two component percentages (60% and 78.57%), weighted by their totals.

Example 4: Theory + practical with unequal weights

Theory: 42 / 60 · Practical: 25 / 40
  1. Theory: 42 / 60 = 70.00%.
  2. Practical: 25 / 40 = 62.50%.
  3. Combined: (42 + 25) / (60 + 40) = 67 / 100 = 67.00%.
  4. Band: B grade (first division). The practical has more influence here than in Example 3, pulling the combined percentage down slightly from the theory score.

Example 5: Whole result (5 subjects)

Sum obtained across subjects: 385 · Sum total: 500
  1. Fraction: 385 / 500 = 0.770.
  2. Percentage: 77.00%.
  3. Band: A grade.
  4. To compute your overall result this way, add every subject's obtained marks and every subject's total marks first, then enter the two sums.

Example 6: Just below a boundary

Obtained: 59 · Total: 100
  1. Fraction: 59 / 100 = 0.59.
  2. Percentage: 59.00%.
  3. Band: C grade (second division, 50–59.99%).
  4. One more mark (60%) would have pushed this into first division — a useful illustration of why boundary marks matter.

Theory vs. practical — how the combination works

When you tick "combine theory and practical", the calculator does not average the two percentages. It adds the obtained marks first, adds the totals, then computes one combined percentage. This is the correct approach because theory and practical papers usually have different maximum marks, and a simple average would give the smaller paper the same weight as the larger one.

  • If theory and practical have equal totals (e.g. 50/50), adding then dividing gives the same answer as averaging. This is a coincidence, not a general rule.
  • If theory is larger (e.g. 70/30 as in Physics), the theory marks dominate the combined percentage.
  • If practical is larger (e.g. 40/60 as in some Chemistry papers), the practical has the greater influence.

This is exactly how BISE boards themselves calculate the final subject mark. A 55/70 theory score and an 18/30 practical score give 73/100 total, not (78.57 + 60)/2 = 69.29.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Swapping obtained and total marks. If the result is greater than 100%, you have almost certainly entered them in the wrong order. The calculator flags this.
  • Entering marks out of the wrong total. Check whether your paper was out of 50, 75, or 100. A common error is assuming every paper is out of 100.
  • Averaging theory and practical percentages directly. Always add the marks first, then divide by the combined total. The two methods give different answers when the totals differ.
  • Forgetting to add practical marks to the obtained total. If you enter only the theory paper in the main fields and then tick combine, you will double-count theory. Use either the main fields OR the theory+practical fields, not both.
  • Rounding too early. Keep full precision until the final percentage. Rounding at intermediate steps can shift the result by 0.01–0.05%, which occasionally matters at division boundaries.
  • Confusing the subject percentage with the aggregate. Your overall result is the total obtained across all subjects divided by the total maximum, not the average of the individual subject percentages (unless all subjects have equal totals).

FAQ

How do I convert marks to a percentage?▾

Divide the obtained marks by the total marks and multiply by 100. For example, if you scored 68 out of 85, the percentage is (68 / 85) × 100 = 80%. The result tells you what fraction of the maximum marks you achieved.

Can I use this for a paper with theory and practical marks?▾

Yes. Tick the Combine theory and practical checkbox. Two extra fields appear. Enter the theory obtained and theory total, then the practical obtained and practical total. The calculator adds the marks first, then computes the combined percentage.

What percentage is a first division in BISE Punjab?▾

For BISE Punjab class 9 and 10, 60% and above is first division, 45% to 59.99% is second division, and 33% to 44.99% is third division. Below 33% is a fail. Individual boards may vary slightly, so check your board's official notification.

What happens if I enter obtained marks greater than the total?▾

The calculator flags this as an error because obtained marks cannot exceed the total. Check your entries — usually either the obtained or the total has been typed incorrectly.

Can I use this for a single subject or for the whole result?▾

Both. For a single subject, enter that subject's obtained and total marks. For the whole result, add up every subject's obtained marks and every subject's total marks first, then enter the two sums.

Why does my school percentage differ slightly from this calculator?▾

Two reasons are common. First, rounding — some schools round to one decimal place, some to the nearest whole number. Second, weighting — some boards weight practicals differently from theory even when the marks are combined. Always use the percentage printed on your official result card as the final authority.
