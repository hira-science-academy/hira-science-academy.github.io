What this final grade calculator does

This tool answers one very specific question: given your current grade, the weight of your final exam, and the overall grade you want, what score do you need on the final? It rearranges the weighted-average equation and solves for the required exam score in one step. Beyond the number, it tells you whether the target is achievable at all — if the required score exceeds 100%, the target is mathematically impossible with those weights, and the calculator shows the highest overall grade you can still reach with a perfect final. If the required score is below 0%, you have already secured the target regardless of how the final goes.

Formula

Required final exam score
Required = Target − Current × (1 − wf) wf
Equivalent form (weighted-average rearrange)
Target = Current × (1 − wf) + Required × wf

Variable definitions

  • Target — the overall grade you want, as a percentage (e.g. 80).
  • Current — your current grade before the final, as a percentage (e.g. 72).
  • wf — the weight of the final exam as a decimal (e.g. 30% → 0.30).
  • Required — the score you need on the final exam, as a percentage.

Worked examples

Example 1: A reachable target

Current grade: 72%, final weight: 30%, target: 80%
  1. Non-final contribution: 72 × (1 − 0.30) = 72 × 0.70 = 50.4.
  2. Points needed from the final: 80 − 50.4 = 29.6.
  3. Required final score: 29.6 / 0.30 = 98.67%.
  4. Result: reachable, but the final must be nearly perfect. The margin is only 1.33 percentage points.

Example 2: Impossible target

Current grade: 85%, final weight: 20%, target: 90%
  1. Non-final contribution: 85 × 0.80 = 68.0.
  2. Points needed from the final: 90 − 68 = 22.0.
  3. Required final score: 22 / 0.20 = 110% — above 100, so impossible.
  4. Highest reachable overall: 68 + 100 × 0.20 = 88%. That is the ceiling even with a perfect final.

Example 3: A reasonable target

Current grade: 60%, final weight: 50%, target: 70%
  1. Non-final contribution: 60 × 0.50 = 30.0.
  2. Points needed from the final: 70 − 30 = 40.0.
  3. Required final score: 40 / 0.50 = 80%.
  4. Result: a strong final can lift a 60% average to 70% overall. Very achievable.

Example 4: Target already secured

Current grade: 78%, final weight: 40%, target: 75%
  1. Non-final contribution: 78 × 0.60 = 46.8.
  2. Points needed from the final: 75 − 46.8 = 28.2.
  3. Required final score: 28.2 / 0.40 = 70.5%.
  4. Result: you can score well below your current average and still hit the target.

Example 5: Target guaranteed regardless of final

Current grade: 92%, final weight: 20%, target: 80%
  1. Non-final contribution: 92 × 0.80 = 73.6.
  2. Points needed from the final: 80 − 73.6 = 6.4.
  3. Required final score: 6.4 / 0.20 = 32% — well below any passing grade.
  4. Even a score of 0 leaves you at 73.6%, still above the target of 80% only if you actually score above the threshold. The calculator will flag this as "target secured with a low final score".

Example 6: BISE Punjab theory + practical combined

Current grade: 65%, final (theory + practical combined): 40%, target: 75%
  1. Non-final contribution: 65 × 0.60 = 39.0.
  2. Points needed from the final: 75 − 39 = 36.0.
  3. Required final score: 36 / 0.40 = 90%.
  4. This 90% is the combined theory + practical score. If theory is 80% of the final and practical is 20%, you would need approximately 90% overall, weighted across both.

How the weights work

The idea behind the final grade calculation is simple: your overall grade is a weighted average of two parts — everything before the final, and the final itself. If the final is worth 30%, then your current grade already accounts for the other 70%. As the final exam weight grows, the required score becomes more sensitive: a small change in the weight can shift the required score by several percentage points.

  • Small final weight (10–20%): the required score is usually close to your target, because the final barely moves the average. Even a weak final often still leaves you close to your current grade.
  • Medium final weight (30–50%): the final has real leverage. A 10-point gap between current and target often translates to a 20–30 point gap in the required final score.
  • Large final weight (60–100%): the final dominates the average. If the final is 100%, your required score is exactly your target — your current grade becomes irrelevant.

This is why "what do I need on the final?" has no single answer — it depends entirely on how much the final is worth in your specific course.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Entering the weight as a whole number instead of a percentage. If the final is worth 30%, enter 30 — not 0.30. The calculator handles the decimal conversion internally.
  • Confusing "current grade" with "last test score". Current grade means your cumulative average across all assessments so far, not just the most recent test.
  • Forgetting that the non-final portion fills the rest of the weight. If the final is 30%, the other 70% is already baked into your current grade. Do not double-count it.
  • Using a letter grade (A, B+) instead of a percentage. Letter grades vary between schools. Convert to a percentage first for a meaningful result.
  • Treating an impossible result as an error. A required score above 100% is a valid result — it simply means the target is not reachable. The calculator explains this instead of showing an error.
  • Treating a negative required score as a bug. It is not. It means your current grade is already above the target, and even a 0 on the final would still leave you above it.
  • Ignoring practical exams in BISE Punjab. If your final includes a practical component, combine the theory and practical marks into a single percentage first, then enter that combined weight.

FAQ

What is a final grade calculator?▾

A final grade calculator tells you the minimum score you need on your final exam to reach a target overall grade. You enter your current grade, the weight of the final exam as a percentage, and the grade you want, and it solves for the required exam score.

What does the final exam weight mean?▾

The weight is how much the final exam counts toward your overall grade, written as a percentage. For example, if your final is worth 30%, the other assessments (tests, assignments, practicals) make up the remaining 70%.

What if the required score is above 100%?▾

If the required score is above 100%, no score on the final can bring you to that target with the given weights. The calculator flags this as an impossible target and shows the highest overall grade you can still reach with a perfect final score.

What if the required score is below 0%?▾

If the required score is below 0%, you have already secured the target regardless of how the final goes. Even a score of 0 on the final would still leave your overall grade above the target. The calculator shows this as a positive outcome, not an error.

Does this calculator work for BISE Punjab class 9 and 10?▾

Yes. It works with any marks or percentage scheme. If your school reports marks out of 100 or as a percentage, use those directly. If your final exam is combined with practicals, first combine them into a single percentage and enter that combined weight.

Should I use my current percentage or my current letter grade?▾

Use the numeric percentage if you have it. Letter grades vary between schools, so converting to a percentage first gives a more accurate required-score result.
