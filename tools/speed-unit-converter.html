What this speed converter does

This tool converts between the five speed units that appear most often in Pakistani school problems and everyday contexts: metres per second (m/s), kilometres per hour (km/h), miles per hour (mph), knots (nautical miles per hour) and feet per second (ft/s). Every conversion goes through the SI base unit (m/s) internally. The article explains the 5/18 factor that every BISE student needs to memorise, the difference between the statute mile used in mph and the nautical mile used in knots, and a real-world reference table with speeds ranging from walking (1.4 m/s) up through the Pakistani motorway limit (120 km/h) to the cruising speed of a PIA jet (900 km/h) and the speed of sound (1234.8 km/h).

The 5/18 factor — why it works

The most important speed conversion in school physics is between km/h and m/s. It reduces to the fraction 5/18 (or its reciprocal, 3.6), and both are worth memorising.

Deriving the factor
1 km/h = 1000 m 3600 s = 5 18 m/s ≈ 0.2778 m/s
The two conversions you need
m/s → km/h: multiply by 3.6
km/h → m/s: multiply by 5/18 (≈ divide by 3.6)

The reason is simple: in one hour there are 3600 seconds, and in one kilometre there are 1000 metres. So 1 m/s means 3600 metres per hour = 3.6 km/h. Reading it the other way, 1 km/h means 1000 metres per 3600 seconds = 5/18 m/s.

orders of magnitude from walking to jet cruise

Worked examples

Example 1: 72 km/h to m/s (the classic)

  1. Multiply by 5/18.
  2. Result = 72 × 5/18 = 72 / 3.6 = 20 m/s.
  3. The most common conversion in BISE motion problems.

Example 2: 20 m/s to km/h (reverse)

  1. Multiply by 3.6.
  2. Result = 20 × 3.6 = 72 km/h.
  3. Confirms the previous example in the other direction.

Example 3: 100 km/h to m/s

  1. 100 × 5/18 = 500 / 18.
  2. Result = 27.78 m/s.
  3. A car at motorway speed covers about 28 metres every second.

Example 4: 8 m/s to km/h (sprinting athlete)

  1. 8 × 3.6.
  2. Result = 28.8 km/h.
  3. The fastest human sprint is about 12 m/s = 43 km/h (Usain Bolt).

Example 5: 60 mph to km/h

  1. 1 mph = 1.609344 km/h.
  2. Result = 60 × 1.609344 = 96.56 km/h.
  3. A common US highway speed — just under the Pakistani motorway limit.

Example 6: 30 knots to km/h (marine / aviation)

  1. 1 knot = 1.852 km/h.
  2. Result = 30 × 1.852 = 55.56 km/h.
  3. A speedboat cruising at 30 knots. A ship's log or aircraft airspeed indicator uses knots.

Example 7: 343 m/s to km/h (speed of sound)

  1. 343 × 3.6.
  2. Result = 1234.8 km/h.
  3. Speed of sound in dry air at 20 °C. Supersonic aircraft fly faster than this (Mach 1 = speed of sound).

Example 8: 32 ft/s to m/s (imperial gravity)

  1. 1 ft = 0.3048 m.
  2. Result = 32 × 0.3048 = 9.754 m/s.
  3. Approximately the acceleration due to gravity in imperial units (32 ft/s² ≈ 9.8 m/s²). This comes up in US or older physics textbooks.

mph vs knots — the nautical difference

Both mph and knots look like "miles per hour", but they use different miles.

Unit Speed in km/h Speed in m/s Mile definition
1 mph (statute)1.6093440.447041609.344 m (statute mile)
1 knot (nautical)1.8520.5144441852 m exactly (nautical mile)

The nautical mile is about 15% longer than the statute mile, so 1 knot is about 15% faster than 1 mph. Aviation and marine navigation use knots because a nautical mile is defined as one minute of latitude on the Earth's surface — so if you know your position in degrees and minutes, distance in nautical miles is straightforward to read off a chart. A typical airliner cruises at about 480 knots (≈ 900 km/h), and fast patrol boats often top out around 40 knots (≈ 74 km/h).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Multiplying by 5/18 when you should divide (or vice versa). km/h → m/s uses × 5/18. m/s → km/h uses × 3.6. Reversing these gives an answer that is off by a factor of 12.96. Remember: m/s is the bigger number for the same physical speed (since 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h).
  • Using 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h but writing it as 1 m/s = 36 km/h. A factor-of-10 slip. 1 m/s is 3.6 km/h, not 36.
  • Confusing mph with knots. mph uses the statute mile (1609.344 m); knots use the nautical mile (1852 m exactly). The difference is about 15%. Aviation and marine navigation use knots; road speeds in the US and UK use mph.
  • Assuming a "mile" in an old textbook is the modern statute mile. Some pre-1959 British textbooks use a slightly different mile. Modern school problems always use the international statute mile = 1609.344 m.
  • Forgetting that ft/s appears in older imperial problems. Gravity in imperial units is 32 ft/s². This comes up in American or older physics texts. Convert to m/s by multiplying by 0.3048.
  • Treating speed and velocity as interchangeable. Speed is a scalar (magnitude only); velocity is a vector (magnitude + direction). This converter handles magnitudes only — it cannot convert negative speeds or directional changes.
  • Mixing km/h with seconds in a distance problem. If speed is in km/h and time in seconds, you must convert one to match the other before multiplying for distance. s = d/t works only when the units are consistent.

FAQ

How do I convert km/h to m/s?▾

Multiply km/h by 5/18, which is the same as dividing by 3.6. For example, 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s. The factor comes from 1000 m per km divided by 3600 s per hour. This is the single most used conversion in BISE physics.

How do I convert m/s to km/h?▾

Multiply m/s by 3.6. For example, 20 m/s = 20 × 3.6 = 72 km/h. This is the inverse of the km/h to m/s conversion and is the more common direction in exam questions because answers to physics problems are often in m/s but road speeds are quoted in km/h.

What is the difference between mph and knots?▾

mph means miles per hour, using the statute mile (1609.344 m). Knots mean nautical miles per hour, using the nautical mile (exactly 1852 m). One knot = 1.852 km/h = 1.15078 mph. Aviation and marine navigation use knots because a nautical mile corresponds to one minute of latitude, simplifying position calculations on charts.

What is a typical Pakistani motorway speed?▾

Pakistani motorways (like the M-2 Lahore–Islamabad) have a speed limit of 120 km/h for cars and 100 km/h for heavy vehicles. Typical cruising speed is 100–120 km/h. City driving in Karachi or Lahore is usually 30–60 km/h with heavy traffic reducing it further.

What is the speed of sound?▾

The speed of sound in dry air at 20 °C is about 343 m/s, which is 1234.8 km/h or about 667 knots. At higher altitudes (where air is colder and thinner), it is slower — around 295 m/s at 11 km altitude. Objects faster than this are supersonic; the speed of sound is also called Mach 1.

Why is 1 m/s equal to 3.6 km/h?▾

In one hour there are 3600 seconds, and in one kilometre there are 1000 metres. So 1 m/s means 1 metre every second, which is 3600 metres every hour, which is 3.6 kilometres every hour. So 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h, and 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s ≈ 0.2778 m/s.
