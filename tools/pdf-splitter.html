    Split a PDF online without installing anything

    A PDF splitter takes one document and turns it into several smaller ones. You might need only the signed page of a long contract, chapter three of a scanned textbook, or a 200-page report cut into sections for different readers. This free PDF splitter does that in your browser. You choose the file, say which pages you want, and download the result a few seconds later.

    Because the work happens on your own device, nothing is sent over the internet and there is no waiting in an upload queue. The tool is built for students, teachers, office staff and anyone who has ever been told “please send only pages 4 to 7”.

    Seven ways to split

    • Extract pages. Type a list such as 1-3, 5, 8-10 to copy those pages into one new PDF.
    • Split by ranges. Type 1-5, 6-10, 11-15 and each range becomes a separate PDF.
    • Every page. One PDF per page, delivered as a ZIP, with each file also available on its own.
    • Split in half. Two parts with the page ranges shown first. An odd page count gives the extra page to part one.
    • Equal parts. Say how many files you want and the pages are shared out as evenly as possible.
    • Every N pages. Fixed-size files, handy for splitting a long PDF into 10- or 20-page chunks.
    • Pick from thumbnails. Scroll through small previews, tick the pages you need, or use Select all, Deselect all, Odd pages and Even pages.

    How to split a PDF in five steps

    1. Drag your PDF onto the drop area, or tap it to choose a file. The file name, size and page count appear straight away.
    2. Choose a splitting method. The options below change to match.
    3. Enter the pages or number of parts. A wrong range, such as page 40 in a 30-page file, shows a clear message and nothing is created.
    4. Press the split button and watch the progress bar.
    5. Download each file with its own button, or press Download all as ZIP. Use Split another PDF to start again.

    The original file is never altered. Your new files are separate copies, named after the original with the page numbers added, such as report-pages-1-3.pdf.

    Everyday uses for splitting PDF pages

    • Study material. Pull one chapter or one past paper out of a big scanned book so it is easy to print or share.
    • Forms and applications. Many portals accept only one or two pages. Extract the page they ask for instead of sending the whole file.
    • Email limits. A long PDF that is too big to attach can be split into two or three parts that each fit.
    • Sharing less. Send a colleague only the pages that concern them and keep the rest private.
    • Scans. Separate a batch scan of several documents into individual files, one range for each.

    Supported files and honest limits

    The tool accepts standard PDF files. It checks that the file really is a PDF, not just that its name ends in .pdf, and it reports empty, damaged and unsupported files instead of failing silently.

    • Size. Files up to 300 MB are accepted, but the practical limit is the memory of your device. A laptop copes with large scans far better than an older phone. If the browser runs out of memory you will see a message suggesting a smaller file.
    • Protected PDFs. Encrypted or password-protected files cannot be opened here. Remove the protection in the program that made the file, if you are allowed to, and try again.
    • Digital signatures. Splitting creates new documents, so a signature on the original does not validate on the pieces. Keep the original signed file as your record.
    • Document-level features. Page content, text, images and graphics are copied. Bookmarks, form fields and links that point to other pages of the original may not survive.
    • Number of files. One run creates at most 1,000 files.

    Privacy: what happens to your PDF

    Splitting runs entirely inside your browser tab. The page downloads a small open-source library (pdf-lib) the first time you choose a file, and, if you use thumbnails, Mozilla’s pdf.js to draw page previews. Your document is not part of any of those requests. When you close or refresh the page, the files held in memory are released.

    The only thing sent to Google Analytics is the name of an action, such as “download”. File names, page counts and contents are never sent. This is a convenience tool, not a place to store documents: if a file is highly sensitive, work on a device you trust.

    Splitting versus the other PDF jobs

    Many people searching for tools such as iLovePDF, PDF2Go or Adobe Acrobat’s split feature want the same outcome: a smaller PDF made from a bigger one. This is an independent tool from Hira Science Academy and is not connected to those products. It focuses on the splitting job and keeps your file on your device.

    When you need the opposite, use the PDF merger to combine files after you have extracted what you need. To mark the pieces as drafts or confidential before you send them, add text or a logo with the PDF watermark tool. If you want pages as pictures, the PDF to image converter saves each page as a JPG, PNG or WebP.

    Splitting does not make a PDF smaller in total, because every page keeps the fonts and pictures it uses. If file size is the problem, shrink the pictures first with the image compressor and rebuild the PDF with Image to PDF.

    Frequently asked questions

    How can I split a PDF file online for free?▾

    Choose your PDF, pick a method such as Extract pages or Split by ranges, enter the pages you want and press the split button. The files are created in your browser and you download them straight away. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no charge.

    Can I split a PDF into individual pages?▾

    Yes. Choose Every page and the tool creates one PDF for each page. You can download any single file, or take them all together in one ZIP file.

    How do I split a large PDF file?▾

    Use Every N pages or Equal parts to break a long document into chunks of a size you choose, for example 20 pages each. Large files are limited by your device’s memory, not by a daily quota. The tool accepts files up to 300 MB, but a phone may struggle with very large PDFs, so a computer works best.

    Can I extract specific pages from a PDF?▾

    Yes. In Extract pages, type the pages and ranges you want, such as 2, 4-6, 9. You can also choose Pick from thumbnails and tick the pages you want to keep. Either way the chosen pages are saved together in a new PDF and the original file is not changed.

    Can I divide a PDF into two equal parts?▾

    Yes. Split in half shows the page ranges of both parts before you start. If the page count is odd, the first part gets the extra page, so a 9-page PDF becomes pages 1–5 and pages 6–9.

    Can I split multiple PDF files at once?▾

    The tool works on one PDF at a time. When you finish, press Split another PDF to start the next one without refreshing the page. To join files instead of dividing them, use the PDF merger.

    Is this PDF splitter free to use?▾

    Yes. It is free with no account, no watermark on the pages and no daily limit on how many files you split.

    Do I need to install software?▾

    No. It runs in your web browser on a computer, tablet or phone. The first time you use it, the browser downloads a small open-source PDF library, so you need an internet connection at that moment.

    Are uploaded PDF files stored on a server?▾

    No. Your PDF is read and split by your own browser and this tool contains no upload code, so the document never reaches our servers. Analytics records only the name of an action, such as a download, never your file name or contents. Like the rest of the site, the page loads Google Analytics and Google advertising scripts; see the Privacy Policy for details.

    Can I split a password-protected PDF?▾

    Not directly. This tool cannot open PDFs that are encrypted or protected with a password, and it says so instead of producing a broken file. If you are allowed to open the document, remove the protection in the program that created it, save a copy, and then split that copy.

    Will splitting change the quality of my pages?▾

    No. Pages are copied across without being re-rendered, so text stays sharp and selectable, and images and vector graphics keep their original quality. Features that belong to the whole document, such as bookmarks, form fields, links between pages and digital signatures, may not carry over to the new files.
