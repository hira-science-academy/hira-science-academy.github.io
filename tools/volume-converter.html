What this volume converter does

This tool converts between the six volume units most commonly seen in Pakistani schoolwork, cooking, medicine and everyday life: litres, millilitres, cubic metres, cubic centimetres, gallons and cubic feet. Every conversion goes through the SI base unit (cubic metre) internally, so the results are exact. The page explains the two most important — and most frequently confused — facts about volume conversion: the cube rule (1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³, not 1000) and the mL/cm³ equivalence (1 mL = 1 cm³ exactly, by definition). It also includes a dedicated section on US vs UK gallons, plus a real-world reference table ranging from a medicine spoon (5 mL) to a Pakistani water tanker (10,000 L).

Why volume factors are cubed

Volume is three-dimensional. A cubic metre is a cube whose side is 1 metre. A cubic centimetre is a cube whose side is 1 centimetre. Since 1 m = 100 cm, a cubic metre is 100 cm along each of three axes, containing 100 × 100 × 100 = 1,000,000 cm³.

why 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ — the linear factor 100 is cubed

The rule in one line

(a × factor)3 = a3 × factor3

If the length factor between two units is f, the volume factor is f³. So while 1 m = 100 cm, the volume relationship is 1 m³ = 100³ cm³ = 1,000,000 cm³. Similarly, 1 ft = 0.3048 m, so 1 ft³ = 0.3048³ m³ = 0.0283168 m³.

The mL/cm³ equivalence

Since 1964, the litre has been defined as exactly 1 cubic decimetre (a cube 10 cm on each side), which contains 1000 cm³. So:

1 L = 1000 mL = 1000 cm³ = 0.001 m³
1 mL = 1 cm³ (exactly, by definition)

This is why a medicine dose of "5 mL" is exactly 5 cm³, why a density of "1 g/mL" is the same as "1 g/cm³", and why a liquid volume in mL is interchangeable with a solid volume in cm³ in any physics problem. This is the single most useful volume equivalence in chemistry and physics.

Worked examples

Example 1: 2 L to mL and cm³

  1. 1 L = 1000 mL.
  2. Result = 2 × 1000 = 2000 mL.
  3. Since 1 mL = 1 cm³, also 2000 cm³.
  4. The classic soda bottle volume.

Example 2: 1 m³ to litres

  1. 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³.
  2. 1 L = 1000 cm³.
  3. Result = 1,000,000 / 1000 = 1000 L.
  4. An industrial container of 1 m³ holds 1000 litres — a common water-tank reference.

Example 3: 500 cm³ to mL

  1. 1 cm³ = 1 mL.
  2. Result = 500 mL.
  3. Half a litre — a standard water bottle size.

Example 4: 1 US gallon to litres

  1. 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 L.
  2. Result = 3.785 L.
  3. This is what fuel economy figures in the US mean.

Example 5: 1 UK gallon to litres

  1. 1 UK (imperial) gallon = 4.54609 L.
  2. Result = 4.546 L.
  3. About 20% larger than the US gallon — a critical difference in imported recipes or fuel specs.

Example 6: 5 mL medicine dose to cm³

  1. 1 mL = 1 cm³.
  2. Result = 5 cm³.
  3. A teaspoon of liquid medicine — the standard unit on a dosing spoon.

Example 7: 10,000 L water tanker to m³

  1. 1 m³ = 1000 L.
  2. Result = 10,000 / 1000 = 10 m³.
  3. A typical large Pakistani water tanker holds 10 m³ — about the volume of a small room.

Example 8: 1 ft³ to litres

  1. 1 ft = 0.3048 m, so 1 ft³ = 0.3048³ = 0.0283168 m³.
  2. 1 m³ = 1000 L.
  3. Result = 0.0283168 × 1000 = 28.317 L.
  4. A cubic foot holds about 28.3 litres — a useful conversion for aquarium or HVAC specs.

US gallon vs UK gallon

The word "gallon" means two different things depending on the country. This is one of the most consequential unit ambiguities in everyday life.

Name In litres In cm³ Where used
US liquid gallon3.7853785.41United States, fuel and liquid measures
US dry gallon4.4054404.88US agriculture (rare)
UK imperial gallon4.5464546.09United Kingdom (historical), some Commonwealth

The UK imperial gallon is about 20% larger than the US liquid gallon. This matters in fuel economy comparisons — "40 miles per gallon" in the US is about 47 miles per gallon in the UK for the same efficiency. In Pakistani contexts, gallons appear mainly in imported recipes, fuel specs for foreign vehicles, and foreign textbooks. When in doubt, assume US gallon unless the source is clearly British.

Pakistan itself uses litres as the standard for all liquid volume — petrol is sold in litres, water tankers are described in litres or gallons (locally, 1 "gallon" typically means the imperial gallon of 4.546 L in older usage, though litres are now standard).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting to cube the linear factor. 1 m = 100 cm, but 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³, not 100 cm³ and not 1000 cm³. The cube rule applies to every volume conversion. The factor between two units is the cube of the corresponding length factor.
  • Confusing US and UK gallons. 1 US gallon = 3.785 L, 1 UK gallon = 4.546 L — a 20% difference. Fuel economy, cooking, and any imported spec must be read with the correct gallon.
  • Assuming litres and cm³ are the same size. 1 L = 1000 cm³, not 1 cm³. A litre is a much larger unit than a cubic centimetre. Similarly, 1 mL = 1 cm³ — the small units match, but the large one does not.
  • Using mL and cm³ as different things in a physics problem. They are interchangeable — 1 mL = 1 cm³ exactly by definition. A chemistry problem that gives you "500 mL of water" is exactly the same as "500 cm³ of water" for density, mass, and volume calculations.
  • Mixing up "cc" and "cm³". In medicine and automotive contexts, "cc" (cubic centimetre) is a common shorthand. It means exactly the same as cm³, and also exactly the same as mL. So "5 cc of medicine" is 5 mL.
  • Confusing dry and liquid gallons. The US has both a liquid gallon (3.785 L) and a dry gallon (4.405 L), used for dry commodities like grain. Recipes and fuel always use the liquid gallon. Agriculture may use the dry gallon.
  • Forgetting that ft³ is much larger than L. 1 ft³ = 28.3 L. A cubic foot looks small but holds over 28 litres. This catches students who assume ft³ and L are comparable in size.

FAQ

How do I convert litres to cubic centimetres?▾

Multiply litres by 1000. For example, 2 L = 2000 cm³. This is exact: 1 L = 1000 mL = 1000 cm³, because 1 mL is defined as exactly 1 cm³. This is the single most useful volume conversion to memorise.

How do I convert cubic metres to litres?▾

Multiply cubic metres by 1000. For example, 2 m³ = 2000 L. Since 1 m = 100 cm, 1 m³ = 100³ = 1,000,000 cm³. And 1,000,000 cm³ ÷ 1000 (since 1 L = 1000 cm³) = 1000 L. So 1 m³ = 1000 L exactly.

Why is 1 m³ equal to 1,000,000 cm³ and not 1000 cm³?▾

Because volume is three-dimensional. Since 1 m = 100 cm, a cubic metre is a cube with 100 cm on each edge. Its volume is 100 × 100 × 100 = 1,000,000 cm³. The linear factor is cubed. Students sometimes write 1000 cm³ by mistake — this would be a cube only 10 cm on each side.

What is the difference between a US gallon and a UK gallon?▾

A US gallon is 3.785411784 litres. A UK (imperial) gallon is 4.54609 litres — about 20% larger. When a fuel or liquid problem mentions 'gallon', check the source: US contexts use the smaller gallon, UK contexts use the larger. Pakistan and most of the world use litres, not gallons.

Is 1 mL exactly equal to 1 cm³?▾

Yes, by definition since 1964. One millilitre is exactly one cubic centimetre. This is why a medicine dose of 5 mL is the same as 5 cm³, and a density of 1 g/mL equals 1 g/cm³. The two units are interchangeable in physics and chemistry.

How many litres are in a Pakistani water tanker?▾

Typical tanker sizes in Pakistani cities range from 2000 L (small) to 10,000 L (large). Water utility tankers used for domestic supply are often around 5000 L. For agriculture, tractors and tankers can carry 3000–6000 L. Since 1 m³ = 1000 L, a 5000 L tanker carries 5 m³ of water.
