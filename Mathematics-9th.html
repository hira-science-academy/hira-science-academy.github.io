Chapter 1 – Real Numbers

Rational and irrational numbers, decimal representation, operations

Updated 2027↓

Real numbers form the foundation of mathematics. This chapter covers rational and irrational numbers, decimal representation, and operations on real numbers.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • Rational number: A number that can be written as \(\tfrac{p}{q}\), where \(p, q\) are integers and \(q \neq 0\).
  • Irrational number: Cannot be written as a simple fraction; decimal form does not repeat or end.
  • Additive inverse of \(a\): \(-a\); Multiplicative inverse: \(\tfrac{1}{a}\) for \(a \neq 0\).
  • \(\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}\) for \(a,b \geq 0\).

Worked Example

Example 1: Show \(0.333\ldots\) is rational. Let \(x = 0.333\ldots\); \(10x - x = 3 \Rightarrow 9x = 3 \Rightarrow x = \tfrac{1}{3}\).

Example 2: Simplify \(\sqrt{50} = \sqrt{25 \times 2} = 5\sqrt{2}\).

Chapter 2 – Logarithms

Laws of logarithms, common logarithms, exponential equations

Updated 2027↓

Logarithms help simplify complex calculations. This chapter covers laws, common logarithms, and applications in solving exponential equations.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • Logarithm: If \(a^x = y\), then \(\log_a y = x\).
  • \(\log_a(mn) = \log_a m + \log_a n\)
  • \(\log_a\!\left(\tfrac{m}{n}\right) = \log_a m - \log_a n\)
  • \(\log_a(m^n) = n\log_a m\)

Worked Example

Example 1: \(\log_2 32 = 5\) because \(2^5 = 32\).

Example 2: Solve \(\log x = 2.5 \Rightarrow x = 10^{2.5} \approx 316.23\).

Chapter 3 – Sets and Functions

Set operations, Venn diagrams, function types, notation

Updated 2027↓

Sets and functions are fundamental concepts in mathematics with applications in computer science and data analysis.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • Set: A well-defined collection of distinct objects.
  • \(A \cup B = \{x \mid x \in A \text{ or } x \in B\}\)
  • \(A \cap B = \{x \mid x \in A \text{ and } x \in B\}\)
  • \(n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)\)

Worked Example

Example 1: \(A=\{1,2,3\}, B=\{3,4,5\} \Rightarrow A\cup B=\{1,2,3,4,5\},\ A\cap B=\{3\}\).

Example 2: \(f(x) = 2x+1 \Rightarrow f(3) = 7\).

Chapter 4 – Factorization and Algebraic Manipulation

Factorization techniques, algebraic fractions, simplification

Updated 2027↓

Factorization simplifies algebraic expressions and equations. This chapter covers various factorization techniques and simplification.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • \((a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2\)
  • \((a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2\)
  • \(a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)\)
  • \(a^3 \pm b^3 = (a \pm b)(a^2 \mp ab + b^2)\)

Worked Example

Example 1: \(x^2 - 9 = (x+3)(x-3)\).

Example 2: \(x^3 + 8 = (x+2)(x^2 - 2x + 4)\).

Chapter 6 – Trigonometry

Trigonometric ratios, identities, triangle solving, applications

Updated 2027↓

Trigonometry deals with relationships between angles and sides of triangles with applications in architecture and navigation.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • \(\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{perpendicular}}{\text{hypotenuse}},\ \cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{base}}{\text{hypotenuse}},\ \tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{perpendicular}}{\text{base}}\)
  • \(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1\)
  • \(1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta\)

Worked Example

Example 1: If \(\sin\theta = \tfrac{3}{5}\), then \(\cos\theta = \tfrac{4}{5}\).

Example 2: \(\tan 45^\circ = 1\).

Chapter 7 – Coordinate Geometry

Cartesian plane, distance formula, midpoint, line equations

Updated 2027↓

Coordinate geometry connects algebra and geometry with applications in computer graphics and engineering.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • \(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\)
  • \(M = \left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\ \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)\)
  • \(m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\)
  • Line: \(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\)

Worked Example

Example 1: \(A(1,2),B(4,6) \Rightarrow d = \sqrt{9+16}=5\).

Example 2: Midpoint of \(A\) and \(B\) is \(M=(2.5,4)\).

Chapter 8 – Logic

Statements, truth tables, logical equivalence, proof techniques

Updated 2027↓

Logic forms the basis of mathematical reasoning and computer programming.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • Statement: A sentence that is either true or false, but not both.
  • \(\sim(p \land q) \equiv \sim p \lor \sim q\)
  • \(\sim(p \lor q) \equiv \sim p \land \sim q\)

Worked Example

Example 1: \(p\) true, \(q\) false \(\Rightarrow p \land q\) is false.

Example 2: \(p\) true, \(q\) false \(\Rightarrow p \lor q\) is true.

Chapter 9 – Similar Figures

Similarity criteria, ratio of areas and volumes, geometry applications

Updated 2027↓

Similar figures have the same shape but different sizes with applications in map reading and scale models.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • Sides of similar figures: ratio \(a : b\)
  • Areas: \(a^2 : b^2\)
  • Volumes: \(a^3 : b^3\)

Worked Example

Example 1: Sides \(2:3\), smaller area \(20\text{ cm}^2\) \(\Rightarrow\) larger area \(= 20 \times \tfrac{9}{4} = 45\text{ cm}^2\).

Example 2: Cubes \(1:2 \Rightarrow\) volumes \(1:8\).

Chapter 11 – Loci and Construction

Geometric constructions, loci problems, engineering applications

Updated 2027↓

Loci are sets of points satisfying specific conditions with applications in engineering design.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • Locus at distance \(r\) from a point: circle of radius \(r\).
  • Locus equidistant from two points: perpendicular bisector.
  • Locus equidistant from two intersecting lines: angle bisectors.

Worked Example

Example 1: Draw perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

Example 2: Circle of radius \(3\text{ cm}\) about \(O\).

Chapter 13 – Probability

Basic concepts, sample spaces, probability calculation, applications

Updated 2027↓

Probability quantifies uncertainty in scientific research and finance.

Key Definitions & Formulas

  • \(P(E) = \dfrac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of possible outcomes}}\)
  • \(0 \leq P(E) \leq 1\)
  • \(P(\text{not } E) = 1 - P(E)\)

Worked Example

Example 1: Die rolled; \(P(\text{even}) = \tfrac{3}{6} = \tfrac{1}{2}\).

Example 2: Coin tossed; \(P(\text{heads}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\).

💡 Study Tips for Class 9 Math

  • • Start with the basics: Review fundamental concepts from previous grades before diving into new chapters.
  • • Practice daily: Solve at least 5 problems from each exercise to build speed and accuracy.
  • • Use the 80/20 rule: Focus 80% of your time on the most important chapters (Logarithms, Trigonometry, Coordinate Geometry) and 20% on easier chapters.
  • • Write clean solutions: Board examiners give marks for neat, step-by-step answers. Show every calculation.
  • • Revise definitions daily: Memorize the definitions and theorems in each chapter—they are key to MCQs and short questions.

🎯 Exam Preparation Strategies

  • • Solve past papers: Practice at least 5 years of past board papers to understand question patterns.
  • • Time management: During the exam, allocate 45 minutes to MCQs, 60 minutes to short questions, and 75 minutes to long questions.
  • • Review your mistakes: After each practice test, analyze your errors and revise the topics you got wrong.
  • • Use the MCQs test: Our interactive MCQs test (see banner above) simulates the actual exam and gives instant feedback.
  • • Revise with our notes: The solved exercises and definitions are your quick revision toolkit—use them in the last week before exams.

⚠️ Common Mistakes Students Make

  • • Skipping steps: Many students jump directly to the final answer, losing marks for method.
  • • Not memorizing formulas: A common reason for errors in Trigonometry, Logarithms, and Coordinate Geometry.
  • • Misreading the question: Always read the problem twice before attempting it.
  • • Overlooking negative signs: A small mistake can change the entire answer.

🧠 Problem-Solving Strategies

  • • Break it down: Divide complex problems into smaller, manageable steps.
  • • Visualize: Draw diagrams or graphs for geometry and coordinate problems.
  • • Check your work: After solving, plug your answer back into the original equation to verify.
  • • Practice variations: Don't just solve the same type of problem—try different approaches.
